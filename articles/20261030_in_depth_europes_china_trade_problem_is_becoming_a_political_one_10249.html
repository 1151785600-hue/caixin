In Depth: Europe’s China Trade Problem Is Becoming a Political One

Caixin Global | FULLTEXT | 709 words | 2026-11-04 10:31

1. Germany’s carmakers previously helped keep EU-China trade disputes in check due to their vast interests in the Chinese market, but this has shifted. VW CEO Oliver Blume now calls for higher EU tariffs on Chinese-made EVs and plug-in hybrids, and Germany—once a strong opponent—is joining France in demanding stronger trade defenses, including a mechanism to swiftly cut off a country’s access to the EU single market.

5. Trade has long served as ballast in the relationship. Between 1975 and 2025, bilateral goods trade grew from less than $3 billion to nearly $800 billion, with the EU running a surplus until 1996 and a deficit since 1997. Starting in 2020, Chinese exports surged while European exports slowed; both fell in 2023, but Chinese shipments rebounded in 2024 while European shipments entered a sustained decline.
