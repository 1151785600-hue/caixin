Hong Kong aims for share of HK$350 billion yacht industry as travel scheme launches

SCMP | FREE | 1307 words | 2026-10-18

Hong Kong can capture a share of the global yacht industry, which is expected to reach HK$350 billion (US$44.6 billion) in six years, a senior official has said as the city launched a scheme to welcome leisure vessels from mainland China.

Secretary for Transport and Logistics Mable Chan also announced fresh measures to attract overseas captains and draw superyachts to the city on Friday.

The new Southbound Travel for Yachts from Guangdong scheme is part of the government’s broader plan to develop Hong Kong’s yacht economy, as outlined in the city’s first five-year plan and the latest annual policy address.

Under the scheme, mainland vessels can register through the Marine Department’s Electronic Business System to sail in Hong Kong waters.

Speaking at the launch ceremony, Deputy Financial Secretary Michael Wong Wai-lun said the global yacht market was expected to reach HK$350 billion by 2032 and that Hong Kong could capture a slice of the market.

He noted that there were about 6,000 superyachts in the world exceeding 30 metres (98.4 feet). More than 90 of them were in Hong Kong and the government planned to roll out more measures to boost the yacht economy.

“The outline of the national 15th five-year plan clearly calls for developing leisure consumption such as cruises and yachts, and Hong Kong’s first five-year plan also stated that we will actively develop it,” he said.

“Our vision for the yacht economy also reflects the global trend.”

Wong said they are also pushing to build four new yacht clubs at the airport, Lamma Island, Aberdeen and Hung Hom, which would provide more than 1,100 berths.

The new yacht clubs would also have four “very attractive” clubhouses to improve and complete the infrastructure and ecosystem of the city’s yacht economy, he added.

Wong also revealed that three of the five registered vessels were from Shenzhen, one was from Zhuhai and one from Guangzhou’s Nansha.

The five vessels all moored at facilities in Discovery Bay provided by Lantau Yacht Club, which organised the launch ceremony.

Transport minister Chan also said the Marine Department would coordinate with local training institutions to offer online courses for overseas captains, with the aim of turning the city into a hub for superyachts.

Chan said the five yachts had varied itineraries, including sea tours and visits to urban areas, and that many of their passengers had booked hotels for stays of three to four days, reflecting “a new model of high-end tourism consumption”.

Director of Marine Wong Sai-fat said on Thursday that 23 individuals or groups had registered under the new travel scheme as of this week.

Max Teng, secretary general of the Shenzhen Boating Industry Association, was one of the five mainland yacht owners arriving on the first day.

He said he came to Hong Kong specifically for an “ice-breaking journey to witness the historic moment”, and would stay only one night in the city.

Teng was cautious, however, about his willingness to return. “Just the cost this time, not counting fuel, we already spent up to HK$40,000,” he said.

Entry and exit clearance fees were 14,500 yuan, he said, which included a processing fee of 2,000 yuan per trip, port berthing fee of 2,000 yuan per hour and customs declaration fee of 1,000 yuan per vessel.

He praised the smooth application through the official system, although he said some technical details still needed work.

“There are some things that don’t quite match, for example, the shore power interfaces,” he said.

“Freedom of navigation is the most important thing for mainland yacht owners.

“Many mainland businessmen buy boats and register companies in Hong Kong for its international endorsement and tax incentives.”

Teng said that he saw the scheme boosting Hong Kong’s yacht trading and benefiting nearby cities such as Shenzhen.

Another Shenzhen yacht owner, surnamed Sheng, said he had come to Hong Kong with several friends for a three-day free and easy trip, and planned to shop in Central and Tsim Sha Tsui.

“Shenzhen is very close to Hong Kong. Now that the government has made the process more automated and standardised, the more convenient it becomes, the more willing we are to come,” he said.

“If this scheme had come into effect before National Day, we might have stayed a couple more days – during holidays the roads are congested, but the sea is not.”

Sheng added that he would be more interested in sailing his yacht to Victoria Harbour to watch the fireworks during holidays.

Franklin Mak, executive vice-president of hospitality at HKR International, the developer behind the Lantau Yacht Club, welcomed the “long-anticipated” scheme, saying the club had prepared for more mainland yachts coming to Hong Kong since 2019.

“We have special rates for our berths, and we purposely reserve space for people who come in on a transient basis, so we can accommodate yachts, unlike other yacht clubs that usually sell all that space to long-term owners,” he said.

Apart from berthing, the club also offers both long- and short-term service package discounts, including hotel accommodation and golf sessions, which are more cost-effective than a long-term membership.

Mak also noted that 10 mainland yachts had registered for the Lantau Yacht Club Boat Show early next month and would start arriving from next week. They represented the largest number in years, up from just two or three previously.

David Norton, commodore of the Royal Hong Kong Yacht Club, said one boat from Zhuhai was set to spend the night at its Causeway Bay headquarters on Friday.

Acknowledging that the club was quite full and could only offer berthing and hospitality on a case-by-case basis, Norton stressed the demand was real.

“There is a big demand in mainland China for participation in water sports, 60 per cent of entries in Hong Kong Race Week at the beginning of the year came from the mainland,” he said, adding he was excited to see if the scheme could bring more mainland entries to water sports events in Hong Kong.

Five designated berthing areas at Tai O, Repulse Bay, Tai Tam Bay, Stanley Bay and Three Fathoms Cove in Sai Kung will provide 115 berths, in addition to those at private yacht clubs.

There are also seven kiosks supplying water to vessels and eight designated bunkering areas, according to the Water Supplies Department and the Marine Department.

While saying the five anchorage areas were well located, Norton noted “there is a lack of facilities” around the areas.

“It’s very hard to get water and find someone to dump some trash or empty the tanks from the toilet,” he said.

He also added that tourists needed an easy way ashore: “They need a landing station, or maybe a water taxi service that will get them on and off the boat. So that’s what’s missing.”

Lawmaker Roy Chu Lap-wai described the registration of five yachts on the first day as “encouraging”.

“In the initial stage, I believe the five yachts that come here will go back and spread the word about how convenient and good Hong Kong is, if everything goes smoothly,” he told a radio programme.

Chu said Hong Kong had many distinctive islands for visiting yachts to explore. He hoped tourists would go island-hopping during the day, then dine and shop in urban areas at night.

“The government also held many successful big events regularly, and they could get more involved in different aspects, so these relatively high-end, big-spending yacht tourists can really spend more money in Hong Kong and contribute to our economy,” he said.

Chu said the city could also develop a “one-day tour” and other services, such as chauffeur-driven cars with professional guides, adding that the Tourism Board could do more to promote these offerings and increase the economic benefits.
