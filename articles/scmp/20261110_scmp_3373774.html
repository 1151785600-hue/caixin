Abby Choi’s ex-brother-in-law tells court he treated her like a ‘biological sibling’

SCMP | FREE | 1101 words | 2026-11-10

The former brother-in-law of slain Hong Kong socialite Abby Choi Tin-fung has said he treated her like a biological sibling and that she had maintained a “very close” relationship with her ex-husband after he absconded ahead of a trial for his alleged role in a HK$6.3 million theft case.

Testifying in the High Court for the first time on Monday, Anthony Kwong Kong-kit also claimed that Choi’s mother had provided his younger brother with a hideout in her native Hainan province after he illegally left Hong Kong on a speedboat in June 2015 to evade criminal proceedings.

The 35-year-old is contesting a joint count of murder for allegedly conspiring to kill the influencer alongside his father Kwong Kau, 68, and his younger brother Alex Kwong Kong-chi, 31, on February 21, 2023.

The Kwong brothers admitted to preventing the burial of the 28-year-old model’s body before the trial began in late September. Kwong Kau, a retired police sergeant, also pleaded guilty to the charge midway through the trial.

Prosecutors contended that Anthony was behind the wheel on February 21, when his brother attacked Choi with a hammer in the back seat of their car. The brothers allegedly led Choi to believe they were on their way to pick up her eight-year-old daughter from her international school in Pak Shek Kok.

Parts of Choi’s dismembered remains, including her head and legs, were discovered in a 600 square foot rental flat in Tai Po’s Lung Mei village three days later.

Opening the defence’s case on Monday, Anthony’s legal counsel, Trevor Beel, highlighted his client’s close relationship with the deceased as he sought to counter accusations that he had clung onto his former in-law’s wealth before the alleged offences.

The court heard Anthony, who said he preferred to be addressed as Damon, had primarily relied on his father’s retirement savings and his mother’s earnings from her boutique business to pursue a degree in the United States between 2011 and 2015.

He said he had planned to stay in the country after completing a two-year economics degree at the University of California, Irvine.

But he was forced to return to Hong Kong in May 2015 after his mother told him his brother had become involved in a theft case, the court heard.

Anthony said he was asked to look after his nephew and niece, who were both infants at the time, in case Alex “went away” to mainland China.

According to Anthony, Choi’s mother, Cheung Yin-fa, made arrangements to pick up Alex and provided him with accommodation in Hainan after he illegally entered the mainland in June 2015.

Anthony said Choi had maintained regular contact with the father of her first two children while being aware that he had been wanted by police. She would talk to him daily through video calls and meet him in person during leisure trips on the mainland, the defendant added.

Anthony said his brother started investing in real estate projects across the border after he became acquainted with people whom he described as “capable and rather wealthy”.

Alex regularly sent part of his investment profits to a Hong Kong money exchange shop, and Anthony would collect it and give it to Choi, the court heard.

The defendant also told the court that Alex and Choi had been supporting their two children financially until 2023 through earnings from investments on the mainland and from reselling luxury watches.

Anthony said he was not close to Choi at first and did not realise she had broken up with his brother in 2015. When she became pregnant the following year, he initially thought the baby belonged to Alex instead of her new partner, Chris Tam Fong-chun.

The defendant said his relationship with Choi became closer after his brother’s departure.

“I did not want the children [between Alex and Choi] to grow without paternal love, so I spent most of my time with them unless I had other engagements with friends,” he said.

“I saw Abby every day and as time went by, month after month and year after year, naturally the family bond developed … The bond between us became like one shared by biological siblings.”

Anthony said his relationship with Choi and Tam became so close that they would go out for meals five days a week, and that he would regularly join gatherings with the couple and their friends, who included company owners and investment bankers.

But he said neither Tam nor the couple’s friends knew his humble background or that he was living at a subsidised flat in Tsuen Wan’s Sheung Man Court.

“Abby cared more about her face. She liked people around her to look relatively wealthy in order not to damage her reputation,” he said.

The influencer also allegedly asked Anthony to lie about his residential address by claiming he was living at the Ocean Pride development near Tsuen Wan West station.

Cheung previously told the court that Anthony coaxed her daughter into funding an egg waffle shop he operated in 2021 and 2022.

Anthony on Monday maintained that the shop in Sham Shui Po was opened at the model’s suggestion to kill time because they both had nothing to do during the Covid-19 pandemic. He said Choi had invested HK$600,000 in the shop, which constantly ran at a loss before he sold it in April 2022.

Questioned why the influencer did not ask her partner Tam to run the business, Anthony said he “did not really work at all”.

He also alleged that Choi’s former chauffeur, Chin Kei-shun, had stolen the store’s profits twice when he was asked to deposit the cash into the shop’s bank account.

In a family meeting with Anthony and his mother, Choi decided against dismissing Chin or reporting the matter to police. The model found Chin was “doing quite well” and that he was always punctual to collect her children from school, Anthony said.

Chin, who gave evidence in an earlier court session, said Choi appeared to run into cash-flow difficulties after she and Tam incurred a HK$5 million (US$637,090) debt during a leisure trip to Macau in August 2021.

Anthony corroborated the driver’s account, saying Choi had borrowed money from him personally while seeking a HK$600,000 loan from his uncle.

Beel pointed out that Choi could have borrowed money from her mother, but the defendant said: “Her mother would borrow money from her, but she would not lend any money to her.”

The trial continues before Deputy Judge Brian Keith and a nine-member jury.
