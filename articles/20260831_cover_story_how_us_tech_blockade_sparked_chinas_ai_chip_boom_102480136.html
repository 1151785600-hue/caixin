Cover Story: How U.S. Tech Blockade Sparked China’s AI Chip Boom

Caixin Global | FULLTEXT | 313 words | 2026-09-02 15:55

2022：

China contributed 26.4% of Nvidia's total revenue.

2025：

Huawei captured a 20.4% market share with 812,000 shipments; internet companies represented nearly two-thirds of China's total AI capital expenditure.

April 2025：

Tightened U.S. export restrictions narrowed Chinese firms' access to advanced AI chips from Nvidia.

Q1 2026：

Domestic AI computing power demand skyrocketed 417% year over year, outpacing 128% growth in supply, per CAICT.

Q2 2026：

Deliveries of Nvidia's H200 chips to Chinese clients accounted for less than 1% of its total data center revenue.

Q2 2026：

SMIC's capacity utilization hit 93.7%, with AI-related chip revenue growing about 40% from the previous quarter.

April 2026：

China Mobile issued a 2.1 billion yuan procurement for AI servers.

April–July 2026：

Leading AI model developers (Z.ai, Moonshot AI, Alibaba) paused or limited new subscriptions due to acute chip shortage.

June 2026：

Tencent's Dowson Tong said no chipmaker has enough capacity to meet market demand; DeepSeek's Liang Wenfeng noted manufacturing capacity will be the core bottleneck for 2026-2027.

July 16, 2026：

Huawei's Ascend-based supernode computing cluster made its offline debut at WAIC in Shanghai.

July 17, 2026：

DFSX's supernode-based intelligent computing cluster displayed at WAIC.

August 2026：

China Mobile awarded bids for its Hohhot intelligent computing center expansion, purchasing thousands of AI server units based on Huawei's ecosystem for about 1.3 billion yuan.

2026：

Z.ai initiated construction of a 1-gigawatt data center slated to use domestic chips exclusively; GLM-5.3-Flash model fully relied on homegrown inference chips during testing.

2026：

The 'GPU Five Little Tigers' (Moore Threads, MetaX, Biren, Iluvatar CoreX, Enflame) sought IPOs.

2026：

JPMorgan projected ByteDance's demand for AI chips would exceed 500,000 units.

2026：

Sunrise launched a new cloud inference GPU using LPDDR memory; secured funding pushing its valuation to 20 billion yuan.

2026：

DFSX launched an AI accelerator card using a domestic 14-nanometer process with compute-in-memory architecture.

2026：

Nano-Core Chip completed series B funding, bringing its valuation to 10 billion yuan, utilizing compute-in-memory architecture.
