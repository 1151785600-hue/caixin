China’s Fixed-Asset Investment Remains Weak as Property Slump Deepens

Caixin Global | FULLTEXT | 517 words | 2026-10-19 12:14

1. China’s fixed-asset investment remained weak during the first nine months of the year, according to data released on Monday. The sluggish performance was characterized by a deepening property slump and continued weakness in infrastructure, which together offset a modest improvement in manufacturing. This dynamic highlights the uneven nature of China’s investment recovery, with traditional sectors struggling while some modern manufacturing segments show resilience.

2. The headline figure for fixed-asset investment fell 7.2% year-on-year for the January-September period, a rate unchanged from the January-August decline. Excluding real estate development, investment fell by 4.2%, which was also unchanged from the previous period. The overall contraction came in slightly smaller than the 7.5% average forecast gathered in a Caixin survey of 10 institutions, suggesting that although investment activity remains tepid, the extent of the decline was marginally better than market expectations.

3. The real estate sector remained the primary drag on investment. Real estate development investment plunged 20.6% in the first nine months, with the decline widening by 0.7 percentage points compared to the first eight months. This sustained contraction reflects the ongoing structural adjustment in China’s property market, which continues to weigh heavily on overall capital formation and economic activity, indicating that the property downturn has yet to bottom out.

4. Infrastructure investment fell 4.2%, with declines deepening in traditional sectors including road transportation, water management, and public facilities management. To counter this trend, policy banks deployed more than 100 billion yuan from an 800-billion-yuan policy-backed financing tool in the first three weeks of September, supporting about 400 projects, according to state media. However, this funding has yet to translate fully into construction activity, limiting its near-term impact on infrastructure investment, as the lag between financing disbursement and actual construction delays any positive effect.

5. Manufacturing investment offered a glimmer of hope amidst the broader weakness. Manufacturing investment fell 2.1% in the first nine months, though the decline narrowed by 0.2 percentage points from the first eight months, signaling a modest improvement. Within manufacturing, investment in electronic equipment manufacturing rose 9.6% year-on-year, while railway, ship, aerospace and other transportation equipment emerged as the fastest-growing manufacturing category, at 15.8%. These pockets of strength suggest that high-tech and advanced manufacturing are continuing to attract capital even as the broader investment climate remains subdued. The resilience in these categories points to a deliberate strategic shift towards future-oriented industries, which may help cushion the overall investment slowdown in the long run.

6. On a seasonally adjusted basis, China’s fixed-asset investment rose 0.07% in September compared to the previous month. This represented a 0.62-percentage-point improvement from August, and importantly, it ended a six-month streak of consecutive declines. While the monthly gain is marginal, it hints at a possible stabilization at the margin, although the persistently negative year-on-year figures suggest that the investment outlook remains challenging without sustained policy support. However, without a sustained recovery in the property sector and a faster conversion of infrastructure funding into physical work, the broader investment climate is likely to remain under pressure in the coming quarters.
