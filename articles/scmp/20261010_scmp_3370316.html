Malaysia budget: healthcare boost, Malay businesses to get more public projects

SCMP | FREE | 1206 words | 2026-10-10

Malaysia’s government will boost spending on social welfare while maintaining subsidies to rein in the cost of living under its 2027 Budget unveiled on Friday, as Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim seeks to shore up public support while under pressure to call for an early general election.

Anwar’s Pakatan Harapan (PH) has suffered bruising defeats in a series of state elections since December, as voters expressed disapproval of his administration’s failure to deliver on promises of reforms, which had helped the ruling coalition to secure the largest share of parliamentary seats in the 2022 polls. Malaysia’s next general election is due by February 2028.

He faces the prospect of presenting possibly the last budget of his term, as his federal partner but state electoral rival Umno demands immediate snap polls, emboldened by its decisive victories over PH in the recent Johor and Negeri Sembilan elections.

Anwar, who is also finance minister, presented an expansionary budget of 460 billion ringgit (US$112.6 billion), with increased spending on education, healthcare and infrastructure along with higher wages and aid for small and medium enterprises (SMEs).

“It must be stressed that the national budget is a moral statement of a responsible government. Malay traditional intelligentsia has long placed justice and trust as the core of governance,” Anwar said when presenting his budget in parliament.

He said the government expected to spend at least 80 billion ringgit (US$19 billion) on subsidies next year. This includes an estimated 40 billion ringgit to sustain fuel subsidies at pump stations and a 6.7 per cent increase in cash handouts to 16 billion ringgit.

The higher subsidy bill takes into account expectations of a prolonged energy crisis caused by the US-Israel war on Iran, which has already forced Malaysia to revise its 2026 budget estimates higher to 444.1 billion ringgit, Anwar said, from the initial target of about 419 billion ringgit.

“We have to remember that the cost-of-living issue is already there even before the Iran war started in February this year,” Irwan Shah Zainal Abidin, a senior associate fellow at Universiti Utara Malaysia’s Economic and Financial Policy Institute, told This Week in Asia.

“Maintaining the fuel subsidies is more about not making things worse rather than addressing the issue of living costs pressure.”

Middle-class Malaysians will benefit from a 1 per cent reduction to their 2027 income tax assessment, and there will be a new tax relief ceiling of 12,000 ringgit to accommodate additional categories including maternity care and elderly care for grandparents, AI subscriptions and vaccination for pets.

The package will offer some relief, but its impact on middle-income purchasing power will be modest, according to Doris Liew, an economist specialising in Southeast Asian development.

Those earning below the taxable threshold would not benefit from the tax relief, while families facing high housing, childcare and eldercare costs could remain under pressure, particularly single-earner households and workers with irregular income, she said.

“For these households, stronger earnings and more affordable essential services matter more than modest tax saving.”

The government will also raise the minimum wage for unskilled workers to 2,000 ringgit from 1,700 ringgit currently from June next year, while the wage floor for semi-skilled workers will be set at 2,500 ringgit for the first time.

Anwar said the government, supported by government-linked firms and major corporations in primary sectors such as banking and oil and gas, had paved the way for more reasonable wages in the country.

“Hence, I urge all private employers to do the same,” he said, adding that companies could only claim tax breaks on salaries paid through bank accounts to close loopholes that had long enabled the hiring of undocumented workers.

To win over the country’s Malay-Muslim majority, the government will expand their business access to nearly 70 billion ringgit worth of small-scale public projects, funding and investments, besides procurement opportunities with national energy giant Petronas and government-linked companies.

The country’s Malays account for 60 per cent of Malaysia’s 34 million population and whose support is vital for political parties to secure legitimacy to rule.

The government will also cut the cost of elderly care, allowing full service tax exemption for annual care centre fees of up to 96,000 ringgit. The government will also allocate 1.3 billion ringgit for cash aid targeted at elderly citizens.

The budget will allocate 1.5 billion ringgit for welfare aid for the disabled, including raising the wage floor by 300 ringgit to 2,000 ringgit for companies claiming benefits for their disabled employees and a 600 ringgit monthly payout for families and caregivers of disabled and bedridden individuals.

“The continued move towards more targeted assistance should help improve the efficiency of government support while reducing leakages. Subsidy reform continues to protect vulnerable households without unduly increasing the cost of living,” said Arshad Mohamed Ismail, group chief executive at local ratings firm Malaysian Rating Corporation.

To boost healthcare, the government will spend 47.7 billion ringgit in 2027 to build and upgrade facilities and improve access to primary and outpatient care and recruit at least 9,000 doctors on a full-time basis in the public health sector.

Education will get a combined budget of nearly 100 billion ringgit, to cover the construction of new schools and food aid for students at primary, secondary and university levels, besides lower repayment requirements for government student loans and tax breaks for companies that help their workers with government-back study loan repayments.

For local businesses, income taxes for small and medium enterprises (SMEs) will be brought down by 1 per cent to help cushion the blow from rising operating costs, while factories can claim sales tax refunds for machinery, spare parts and equipment sourced from local traders or distributors.

The Malaysian central bank will also offer an additional 5 billion ringgit in funding in 2027 for SMEs affected by the Iran war, adding to the 5 billion ringgit set aside for this year.

A total of 6 billion ringgit will be set aside to develop tech capabilities among Malaysians, which includes providing 100,000 free subscriptions to new AI applications on the market.

In an uncharacteristically blunt assessment of its own performance, the government said in its 2027 Economic Outlook Report that it had fallen short of delivering on promises to keep prices low, narrow the wage gap and improve healthcare access.

The report, released ahead of Friday’s budget, cited limited welfare coverage for self-employed and informal workers, fragmented delivery of targeted cash aid, substandard student performance, a chronic shortage of specialist doctors and escalating medical costs.

“Despite significant progress, Malaysia’s social protection landscape continues to face challenges in providing adequate and comprehensive protection across different forms of employment and household circumstances amid the rising cost of living,” the report said.

The government said it was on track to narrow the country’s deficit to 3.3 per cent next year through more efficient subsidy mechanisms, improving on the revised target of 3.6 per cent for 2026.

Inflation is expected to range between 1.8 and 2.8 per cent in 2027, likely tracking higher in the first half of the year due to carry-over effects from higher energy, food and other input costs before easing as the year progresses.

The economy is forecast to grow by 4.8-5.3 per cent this year before moderating slightly to 4.2-5.2 per cent in 2027, buoyed by resilient domestic demand and sustained global demand for Malaysia’s semiconductors and AI-related products.
