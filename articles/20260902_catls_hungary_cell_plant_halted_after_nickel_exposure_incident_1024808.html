CATL’s Hungary Cell Plant Halted After Nickel Exposure Incident

Caixin Global | FULLTEXT | 328 words | 2026-09-08 10:39

Contemporary Amperex Technology Co. Ltd.

Contemporary Amperex Technology Co. Ltd. (CATL) faces suspended cell production at its Hungarian plant after worker nickel exposure. Regulators demand safety fixes; CATL invested €7.34 billion in Debrecen. It was fined for environmental violations, and Hungary's new government tightened scrutiny. CATL supplies Mercedes-Benz from other facilities at extra cost, causing delays.

Mercedes-Benz

According to the article, Mercedes-Benz is a customer of CATL. Due to the suspension of CATL’s cell production in Hungary, CATL is supplying Mercedes-Benz from other facilities at additional cost.

BMW

According to the article, BMW production at CATL's Hungarian battery plant has not yet begun. The cell production line was temporarily suspended due to safety deficiencies, causing delays that affect supply to customers like BMW.

Samsung

According to the article, Hungary's transport and investment minister said on Aug. 16 that major foreign investments, including projects by Samsung, would no longer be exempt from environmental and urban-planning rules. Under the new approach, companies found in violation would be unable to obtain new permits for at least six months.

SK On

SK On (SK On) is a company referenced in the article as having major foreign investments in Hungary. Under new Hungarian government policy announced August 16, projects by SK On, CATL, and Samsung will no longer be exempt from environmental and urban-planning rules; violators cannot obtain new permits for at least six months.

Yunnan Energy New Material Co. Ltd.

Yunnan Energy New Material Co. Ltd., a battery separator maker, had its Hungarian subsidiary's operations suspended in June after groundwater contamination. This followed stricter oversight in Hungary after the 2026 political shift.

BYD Co. Ltd.

According to the article, BYD Co. Ltd.'s passenger-car plant being built in Hungary was investigated and fined 10 million forints for improper soil dumping. Its start of operations was pushed back from the fourth quarter of 2025 to late 2026, amid stricter oversight of Chinese manufacturers following Hungary's political shift.
