Xi projects confidence in National Day speech, with rallying call for economic growth

SCMP | FREE | 1067 words | 2026-10-01

Chinese President Xi Jinping projected a message of confidence about the country’s ability to overcome challenges during his annual National Day address, issuing a rallying call for robust economic growth and more certainty amid geopolitical complications.

Xi’s address, delivered at a state reception in Beijing on Wednesday evening to mark the 77th anniversary of the founding of the People’s Republic, was his first to the nation following his state visit to the US last week. It came weeks ahead of a key Communist Party conclave next month.

In a resolute tone projecting confidence about the nation’s growth trajectory despite a complex global environment, Xi’s speech included a forceful push for economic resilience and stability.

“We are extremely proud of the world-renowned achievements of New China and full of confidence in the bright future of Chinese-style modernisation,” he said, according to state news agency Xinhua.

Noting that this year marked the beginning of the 15th five-year plan, which covers 2026-2030 and is crucial for cementing China’s footing in the global tech race and advanced industrial chain, Xi called for steady approaches to “reform and opening up” while striking a balance between growth and national security.

“Our country’s economy has forged ahead under pressure, shifting towards new drivers and higher quality, and demonstrating strong resilience and vitality,” he said.

Acknowledging China was facing “a complex and intricate domestic and international landscape”, he ordered the Chinese government to “strive to meet the year’s targets and tasks”, and ensure a strong start for the five-year plan.

He also pledged to “unswervingly” implement the “one country, two systems” principle and better support Hong Kong and Macau in integrating into China’s overall national development and long-term stability and prosperity.

On cross-strait relations, the Chinese leader maintained a tough line. Xi reiterated Beijing’s pledge to “resolutely combat ‘Taiwan independence’ separatist forces” and to “oppose interference by external forces”.

But he also explicitly called for efforts to “promote the peaceful development of cross-strait relations and advance the great cause of national reunification”. This specific phrasing was not part of his National Day address last year, when cross-strait tensions were high.

Beijing views Taiwan as part of China to be reunited by force if necessary, and considers it the core of its core interests. Most countries, including the US, do not acknowledge the self-ruled island as an independent state, but Washington is opposed to any attempt to take the island by force and is legally committed to supplying it with arms.

The fifth plenary session of the party’s 20th Central Committee will be held from October 26 to 29. It is the most important gathering ahead of next autumn’s national party congress, a five-yearly event marked by top-level reshuffles. The party is also gearing up for a seamless leadership transition next year.

At the fifth plenum, officials will discuss and approve a draft policy decision by the Central Committee to strengthen party governance and discipline. Consultations on the document have already been held, and it will be submitted to the Central Committee for final deliberation and approval, according to a Politburo meeting held last week.

At the reception, Xi also said China would continue to adhere to an independent foreign policy of peace to “inject greater certainty and positive energy into an uncertain and turbulent world”, according to Xinhua.

The recent warming of US-China ties, marked by Xi’s state visit and commitments for two more summits this year, has injected renewed certainty into what had been a deeply strained relationship. The two leaders will meet again at the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (Apec) summit in Shenzhen in November and the Group of 20 (G20) meeting in Miami in December.

“Chinese and American leaders meeting four times within a year is unprecedented in the history of US-China engagement,” said Xin Qiang, deputy director of the Centre for American Studies at Fudan University.

“Although the two powers have conflicting interests and structural frictions, they remain willing to meet and talk, seeking ways to manage their ties,” Xin said.

He suggested the detente could provide Beijing with a strategic window from late this year to early next year if ties remained calm.

Yet cordiality should not obscure the fundamental reality: competition in key areas such as artificial intelligence (AI) remains intense. US President Donald Trump said on Tuesday that he did not want to work with China to address AI risks, adding that cooperation might undermine America’s lead in the field.

Beijing, on the other hand, has unveiled detailed innovation action plans for the next five years, emphasising frontier areas such as AI, the life sciences and biotechnology, controlled nuclear fusion and talent training.

China’s economy is also at a critical point. Amid an intensified push for tech and innovation, Beijing faces domestic pressure to step up job creation, especially for young people, and incentivise investment and consumption.

Marking a shift in its assessment of the country’s economic performance, Beijing on Monday acknowledged emerging challenges in the economy and signalled that more policies were in the pipeline to revive faltering growth.

This was quickly followed by new supportive measures on Tuesday from the Chinese central bank and Ministry of Finance, including a new subsidy for the interest on home mortgages.

“Persistently weak domestic demand has this year been aggravated by the energy-supply shock from the Iran conflict,” said Gavekal Dragonomics in a research note on Wednesday.

“The artificial intelligence boom is lifting China’s exports, but there has not been much spillover into stronger domestic investment or consumption.”

Concluding his address with a sweeping declaration on the country’s trajectory, Xi proclaimed that the great rejuvenation of China was “unstoppable”.

“After a long period of exploration and practice, the socialist system with Chinese characteristics has demonstrated tremendous advantages, and the modernisation of our country’s governance system and capacity has continuously improved,” he said, according to Xinhua.

He urged the nation to “firm up our confidence, lock on to our goals and move forward steadily step by step” to realise China’s success.

Xi and other party and state leaders attended a ceremony to present flowers to fallen national heroes at Tiananmen Square in Beijing on Wednesday morning, marking Martyrs’ Day, which commemorates those who gave their lives for national independence and prosperity.
