Nature Retracts Two Papers by Chinese Scientists Following Blogger’s Fraud Crusade

Caixin Global | FULLTEXT | 620 words | 2026-08-01 02:27

1. A campaign launched by a prominent Chinese science blogger, known by the handle "Geng Tongxue Jiang Gushi", has led to severe repercussions, resulting in the retraction of two research papers in top-tier Nature journals on July 30.

[para.

1

]

These retractions followed an earlier correction issued by Nature Cell Biology for another paper flagged by the same blogger.

[para.

]

Out of the studies publicly questioned by the online sleuth, two have now been retracted and one corrected.

[para.

2

]

2. The two retracted papers originated from research teams at Tongji University and Nankai University.

[para.

3

]

Both studies faced allegations of abnormally similar raw data shortly after their publication.

[para.

3

]

During subsequent investigations, journal editors discovered further data discrepancies and ultimately concluded that the experimental data could no longer be trusted.

[para.

Earlier, a team from East China Normal University had corrected five source-data errors in their paper following an author review.

[para.

3. In January 2025, Wang Ping, former dean of Tongji University's School of Life Sciences and Technology, and researcher Jin Jiali published a study in *Nature* on human HDAC6 regulating DNA damage.

5

The blogger released several in-depth videos in late March and early April of 2026 alleging fraud, dubbing the study "the fakest Nature paper in history" and rebutting Jin's previous explanations on PubPeer.

Tongji University announced a special investigation on April 16.

[para.

8

A week later, *Nature* added an editorial note alerting readers to data reliability concerns.

By May 6, the university dismissed Wang as dean and terminated Jin's employment for severe academic misconduct.

The July 30 *Nature* retraction notice stated that post-publication reviews raised concerns over the authenticity of original numerical data, which appeared unusually similar both within and across figures.

[para.

Editors found further issues in the extended data figures and deemed the authors' justifications for the data similarities unpersuasive, stating the scope of integrity concerns could not be resolved.
