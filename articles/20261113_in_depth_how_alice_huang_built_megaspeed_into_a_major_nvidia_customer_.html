In Depth: How Alice Huang Built Megaspeed Into a Major Nvidia Customer

Caixin Global | FULLTEXT | 804 words | 2026-11-13 10:36

1. Alice Huang, a Chinese businesswoman who had an affair with former Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang, built a Singapore-based computing business whose reported assets surged from just $75 to nearly $3 billion in three years, while creating a network of data center projects in China. Her dealings are now under investigation, exposing the opaque ownership behind a fast-growing supplier of AI computing power. Singapore's Ministry of Home Affairs stated on Thursday that police had warned Alice Huang for knowingly providing inaccurate information about her role at Megaspeed International Pte. Ltd., a Singapore data center company and Nvidia customer, and barred her from returning to the country. Authorities found insufficient evidence to establish her involvement in suspected false declarations over server purchases, and stressed she had not been investigated for suspected espionage despite allegations of intelligence ties.

2. Alice Huang was CEO and chairwoman of Megaspeed and one of its principal actual controllers. Her business trajectory combines several forces reshaping China's technology industry: Washington's restrictions on Chinese companies' access to advanced U.S. chips, the migration of Chinese computing demand to Southeast Asia, and China's ambitious AI infrastructure projects backed by local governments.

3. Singapore's police and customs began an independent investigation into Megaspeed in February 2025 over suspected offenses, including false declarations during server procurement. On June 24, 2025, the U.S. Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security conducted a voluntary post-shipment verification of goods supplied to Megaspeed, marking the first time Singapore learned U.S. officials were examining the company. In February 2026, after consulting the Attorney-General's Chambers, police concluded evidence was insufficient to establish Alice Huang's involvement in possible false declarations but issued a stern warning for knowingly supplying inaccurate information. Nvidia's board also began an independent investigation, while President Donald Trump said the FBI was looking into the relationship between Jensen Huang and Alice Huang, whom intelligence officials suspect is a Chinese spy.

4. Alice Huang is originally from Xiangtan in Hunan province and holds Hong Kong residency. About two decades ago, she studied for a master's degree in media management at Hong Kong Baptist University, then worked at China Central Television and Hunan Television before moving into finance with junior positions at HSBC and Citic Securities. In 2015, she became a core shareholder at Shanghai Hexi Investment Management, and joined Megaspeed in 2023, though her connections stretched back further. Megaspeed began as a gaming company, incorporated in September 2015 as 7Road International, the overseas operating entity of gaming giant 7Road Holdings. In 2015, Alice Huang participated in the acquisition of 7Road Holdings by Zhejiang Century Huatong Group, and in March 2023, all 7Road International shares were transferred to Huang Le Ltd., controlled by Alice Huang, with the company later renamed Megaspeed International.

5. About eight months later, all Megaspeed shares were transferred to another Singapore company, Swiftdata, incorporated on Oct. 31, 2023, with sole shareholder and director Singapore citizen Tan Yong Pong. Reports described Swiftdata as a typical nominee shareholding vehicle, with the Wall Street Journal reporting Alice Huang as the actual controller. On Nov. 14, 2023, Megaspeed became a Nvidia Cloud Partner and developed into one of Nvidia's most important Southeast Asian trading partners. Bloomberg reported Megaspeed imported at least $4.6 billion of Nvidia equipment between 2023 and November 2025, involving at least 136,000 GPUs. Corporate filings show assets increased from $75 in 2022 to $32.9 million in 2023 and about $3 billion in 2024, while revenue reached $294 million in 2024 after being zero in prior years.

6. As the global AI boom accelerated in 2024, Chinese businesses faced U.S. restrictions that effectively halted direct exports of high-end GPUs to China, prompting Chinese computing companies to establish overseas entities and obtain computing power abroad. Southeast Asia became a major investment destination due to geopolitical stability and stable electricity supplies. An industry insider said Chinese-backed data centers accounted for 60% to 70% of approved projects in Thailand, with Chinese tech giants like ByteDance, Alibaba, and Tencent leasing data centers in the region. Megaspeed took advantage of the boom, becoming Nvidia's largest distributor in Southeast Asia, with three data centers in the region.

7. Alice Huang also bet big on China's data center business. In 2021, Century Huatong and Alice Huang-linked Wuxi Feiye Investment jointly established Shanghai Longteng for a 5G data center project in Shenzhen. In June 2022, Wuhu's government signed an agreement with Shanghai Longteng for an intelligent computing industrial park with planned investment of about 32.6 billion yuan. In May 2024, another agreement was signed with Liangshan Yi autonomous prefecture for an AI data center with planned investment of 35 billion yuan. At the end of 2024, a 6 billion yuan intelligent computing center project was agreed with Yancheng. Public information shows none of the three projects has fully been put into operation.
