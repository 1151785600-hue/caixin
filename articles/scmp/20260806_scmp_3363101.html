China’s retaliation against US shows depth of its trade war strategy, analysts say

SCMP | FREE | 1048 words | 2026-08-06

China’s latest export controls and sanctions against American companies in response to recent US measures underscore Beijing’s expanding economic toolkit and its growing willingness to deploy it, according to analysts.

“The multiple actions that China undertook underscore the breadth and depth of China’s retaliatory toolbox,” said Wendy Cutler, the senior vice-president at the Asia Society Policy Institute.

She added that Beijing’s latest response stood in “sharp contrast” to its “ability and willingness” to retaliate during US President Donald Trump’s first term.

The Chinese Ministry of Commerce on Wednesday announced a series of retaliatory measures, including tighter export controls on drones and related technologies, sanctions against seven US companies, and the launch of its first foreign trade-related national security investigation.

“China’s countermeasures have been restrained overall. China values the hard-won stability of China-US economic and trade relations. We hope the US side can work with China in the same direction,” the commerce ministry said in a statement.

The ministry highlighted that the measures were in response to recent US actions, including the sanctioning of 43 Chinese companies under a forced labour law and the Federal Communications Commission’s (FCC) bans on imports of new Chinese drones, robots, and power inverters.

The head of Arizona-based Compliance Testing LLC, one of the sanctioned entities, said China’s actions came as “a bit of a surprise” to him, but asserted that his firm’s work supports US security interests.

“This has to do with our national security,” Michael Schafer, the President of Compliance Testing LLC told the South China Morning Post.

“There are critical products which are manufactured and also tested in foreign adversary countries or non-reciprocal countries that we don’t have trading arrangements with.”

The firm is an FCC-recognised testing laboratory and telecommunications certification body that provides testing services for electronic products. It was targeted for assisting the federal agency in introducing measures that Beijing said endanger “China’s sovereignty, security and development interests”.

In April, the FCC advanced a proposal to ban Chinese labs from testing electronic devices, a move that has now drawn a response from Beijing. According to the FCC, around 75 per cent of all US electronics are tested in China.

China broadens its economic arsenal of legal, regulatory measures

Since 2020, China has broadened its economic toolkit, introducing a growing array of legal, regulatory and trade measures aimed at responding more forcefully to US restrictions and reducing the asymmetry in available policy tools.

Beijing’s legal architecture now includes laws and regulations such as the Export Control Law, Unreliable Entity List, Anti-Foreign Sanctions Law and Foreign Trade Law.

“They reveal that China’s countermeasures framework and toolkit have come of age,” said Sourabh Gupta, a resident senior fellow at the Institute for China-America Studies.

The most ambitious use of Beijing’s export controls came in April 2025, when China imposed sweeping restrictions on seven rare earths, triggering supply disruptions and temporary factory shutdowns around the world. A US-China truce in October last year helped avert a full-blown crisis.

“Beijing is signalling it has an expanding toolkit and is willing to use it, while leaving room to de-escalate,” Kyle Sullivan, vice-president at the US-China Business Council, wrote in a social media post.

Sullivan also pointed to the first use of the national security investigation mechanism under China’s Foreign Trade Law, targeting imported printing and copying office equipment that uses foreign-made software. US technology companies such as Microsoft and HP are among the major players in the printing software market.

“The broader package also looks more calibrated and coordinated than some past retaliation,” he added.

“It suggests China is building a more diversified toolkit for responding to US economic-security measures rather than relying on ad hoc retaliation.”

A senior US government official on Wednesday criticised China’s latest sanctions, warning that Beijing’s actions risk derailing efforts to stabilise economic ties between the world’s two largest economies.

“We are deeply disappointed that China has chosen to announce another round of escalatory measures,” the official said in a statement to the SCMP.

“Over the past two months, Beijing has taken a series of actions that undermine US economic interests and make it more difficult to achieve the stable and balanced economic relationship that both countries should seek.”

The official said that while the Trump administration and China’s leadership maintain a close relationship, “expanding the cycle of retaliation will not produce a durable solution.”

“President Trump will not allow American workers and manufacturers to continue bearing the costs of those practices,” the official added.

The US State Department also blamed China’s actions, saying they have disadvantaged US businesses and harmed American workers.

“China’s tactics put American businesses at a disadvantage and displace American workers,” a State Department spokesperson told the SCMP.

Tit-for-tat escalation fails to derail Trump-Xi summit in September

The latest measures mark another round of tit-for-tat escalation in recent days, with the US imposing tariffs and both Washington and Beijing expanding export controls and restrictions targeting companies.

While each side has blamed the other for the moves, they have so far avoided allowing the disputes to derail preparations for Chinese President Xi Jinping’s expected visit to the US in September.

Last week, China’s Vice-Premier He Lifeng held a video call with US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and US Trade Representative Jamieson Greer to finalise economic deliverables for the visit, while also raising concerns about each other’s measures.

“It’s doubtful that the US will take further actions in response to the latest Chinese countermeasures,” Cutler said, but added that Washington’s response today had put Beijing on notice that “stabilisation should not be taken for granted.”

However, Sullivan highlighted that despite the breadth of China’s measures, the “response looks fairly restrained.”

“Beijing had considerably more painful options available and didn’t use them.”

Gupta of the Institute for China-America Studies noted that China’s toolkit is “no longer just ornamental but is fit for purpose”.

“This past half-decade will come to be remembered many decades hence as the seminal moment that launched the modern age of China’s economic statecraft,” Gupta said.
