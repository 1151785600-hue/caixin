Analysis: China’s Banks Move AI From Pilots to Production

Caixin Global | FULLTEXT | 605 words | 2026-11-11 13:38

1. China's financial industry is moving beyond experimental AI pilot projects, with banks now deploying the technology to automate routine operations, accelerate research, and build systems capable of executing complex tasks.

2. However, broader adoption presents difficult questions for financial institutions, including whether productivity gains justify costs, how to integrate AI with fragmented data systems, and how to defend against increasingly sophisticated cyberattacks.

Reported usage figures illustrate the shift: Bank of Jiangsu said AI applications have cumulatively replaced 1.2 million working hours; Shanghai Pudong Development Bank said its digital workforce handled workloads equivalent to more than 2,500 people annually; and Bank of China's systems can recognize 305 types of documents across 3,800 business scenarios.

The most effective current applications involve repetitive, rules-based tasks or those dependent on processing large volumes of information, including document handling, customer service, payment-fraud detection, and preliminary credit assessments. Bank of Jiangsu reported that AI reduced the time required to approve credit for small and midsize enterprises by 30%.

5. Investment research presents a more complicated challenge. While AI can collect information, analyze financial statements, screen securities, and produce reports—reducing time analysts spend on preliminary work—it cannot necessarily make reliable investment judgments. Financial insiders said AI-generated research may serve as a reference but is not automatically suitable for external publication or decisions involving significant financial risk. Human analysts remain responsible for interpreting results and exercising judgment.

6. The next phase of adoption involves AI agents capable of performing several connected tasks rather than responding to a single prompt. For instance, an agent could retrieve data, compare companies, screen investment products, and assemble an initial report. Citic Securities has developed proprietary AI for ETF screening, stock analysis, and financial statement analysis. Moonshot's Kimi has been deployed at institutions including ICBC, CICC, Citic Securities, and E Fund Management.

8. Building such capabilities is expensive, particularly for institutions wanting to keep sensitive data within their own systems. According to a source at Tencent's WorkBuddy platform, a single private AI project can cost several million to tens of millions of yuan, excluding ongoing maintenance. Private systems offer greater control but may take around three months to update, whereas SaaS solutions can be updated in roughly two days, though with security and data-management implications.

An AI technology executive at a joint-stock bank noted the lack of a bridge between business and technology teams: business staff don't understand the technology, while technical staff don't fully understand business needs.
