Abby Choi called ex-father-in-law ‘old crook’ amid HK$72m flat row, court hears

SCMP | FREE | 1018 words | 2026-10-07

Hong Kong socialite Abby Choi Tin-fung called her former father-in-law an “old crook” as she accused her ex-relatives of turning her children against her when she tried sell a HK$72 million (US$9.17 million) flat, a court has heard.

Choi’s mother, Cheung Yin-fa, on Tuesday said her daughter had become hostile towards her former parents-in-law, Kwong Kau and Jenny Li Sui-heung, in the months before the influencer’s death in February 2023.

Cheung wept in the witness box as she watched footage of her late daughter welcoming guests to the luxury flat, prompting the judge to temporarily halt the trial.

“My daughter was a really good daughter, a really filial daughter,” Cheung said tearfully. “She trusted and gave her heart to the wrong people. This family, this family really bit the hand that fed them.”

Choi’s ex-husband, Alex Kwong Kong-chi, 31, his father, Kwong Kau, 68, and his elder brother, Anthony Kwong Kong-kit, 35, are standing trial at the High Court after pleading not guilty to murdering the model on February 21, 2023.

Kwong Kau, a retired police sergeant, also denied preventing the lawful burial of Choi’s body between February 21 and 23. His two sons pleaded guilty to that offence before the trial.

The killing allegedly stemmed from a family feud over the proposed sale of the HK$72 million flat at the Kadooria residential complex in Ho Man Tin. Kwong Kau, the property’s registered owner, allegedly resented Choi’s attempts to sell it and enlisted his two sons to help kill her so he could keep it for himself.

Cheung said on Monday that her daughter had bought the property in 2019 for her children and partly as a personal investment, but she registered it in her former father-in-law’s name to avoid double stamp duty. She stressed that Choi had never intended to give the flat to her former in-laws.

Giving evidence for a second day, Cheung said Kwong Kau became furious when her daughter first told him in June 2022 that she intended to sell the flat. He threatened to kill her entire family if she went ahead, Cheung said.

The elder Kwong signed a power of attorney authorising Choi to negotiate the sale only five months later, after Cheung threatened to sue him.

Choi aired her grievances in a series of audio messages to her mother in October and November 2022, saying her former in-laws were ungrateful despite her having supported their lavish lifestyle for years.

“She does not cherish her blessings at all,” Choi said in an apparent reference to Li, who is not a defendant in the case. “[They are] not grateful to me even while leading such a lifestyle.”

The influencer also expressed regret at having registered a property in Hainan province and a Rolls-Royce in Kwong Kau’s name.

Cheung said Kwong Kau and his wife also disparaged her daughter in front of the two children she had with Alex Kwong and told them not to listen to their mother.

The court heard that Choi and her mother had further discussions in November 2022 about how to get Kwong Kau to sign legal documents authorising her to negotiate the sale.

Choi suggested that the elder Kwong, whom she called an “old crook”, “scum” and a “stinking bastard”, was deliberately delaying the process and would play tricks on them unless the matter was settled quickly, the court heard

Cheung said her daughter wanted to sell the property as soon as possible and was willing to lower her asking price by a few million dollars because Kwong Kau could revoke the power of attorney at any time.

The model also complained that the Kwong family had prevented her from speaking to her two children by phone while she was attending a fashion event in France during the Lunar New Year holiday in January 2023.

Cheung told the court she received her final message from her daughter at around 2pm on February 21, saying she would pick up her daughter from school.

Fear for the model’s safety grew that night when Choi’s husband, Chris Tam Fong-chun, informed Cheung that she had gone missing.

Cheung said she called Anthony Kwong, who was working as Choi’s temporary chauffeur at the time, but he appeared impatient and insisted the influencer had got out of her car after an argument over the phone.

She said her former relative gave conflicting answers when pressed to explain Choi’s whereabouts. He also allegedly suggested that she might have got drunk and become lost, even though she normally avoided alcohol.

Cheung said she and Tam reported the case to police shortly before midnight, much to Alex Kwong’s dismay.

“He said: ‘Damn you! Why didn’t you alert me when you called police?’” she said.

“I said to him: “Damn you! I reported my daughter’s disappearance to police. What does it have to do with you?”

When Cheung suggested that Alex Kwong and his father must have taken Choi somewhere, her former son-in-law replied with a question: “Why did you say so?”

“I called the whole [Kwong] family but nobody cared,” the witness added.

Defence counsel Trevor Beel, representing Anthony Kwong, argued the elder Kwong was reluctant to sell the flat during the economic slump linked to the Covid-19 pandemic.

“I suggest, madam, that it would be understandable that Kwong Kau would not be keen to sell the property at that time, because the property price was low,” he said in cross-examination.

Cheung disagreed.

“The money wasn’t his. What did he stand to gain? We were entitled to reclaim [the flat]. He had no say on it,” she said.

Some members of the public burst into laughter when Cheung recalled her exchange with Alex Kwong, drawing a stern warning from lead prosecutor Charlotte Draycott SC.

“It’s important for the public to remember that this is a woman who has lost her daughter,” she said. “If you want to laugh, you go outside.”

The trial continues before Deputy Judge Brian Keith and a nine-member jury.
