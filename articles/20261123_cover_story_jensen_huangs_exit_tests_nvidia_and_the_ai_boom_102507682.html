Cover Story: Jensen Huang’s Exit Tests Nvidia — and the AI Boom

Caixin Global | FULLTEXT | 1109 words | 2026-11-26 11:56

Nvidia

Nvidia, the AI-computing giant co-founded by Jensen Huang, lost its CEO of 33 years when he resigned Nov. 12 after an extramarital affair. The company now faces U.S.-China chip export scrutiny, overseas sales investigations, and questions over its customer-financing model. CFO Colette Kress is interim CEO, with revenue still surging 103% year-over-year.

Megaspeed

Megaspeed is a Singapore-based data center company, formerly an overseas subsidiary of Chinese gaming firm 7Road Holdings. Transferred in March 2023 to a company controlled by Alice Huang, it became a certified Nvidia cloud partner. Its assets grew from $75 in 2022 to nearly $3 billion in 2024, drawing scrutiny over alleged diversion of restricted Nvidia chips, though it denies wrongdoing.

7Road Holdings Ltd.

According to the article, 7Road Holdings Ltd. (第七大道) is a Chinese gaming company. Its overseas subsidiary was transferred in March 2023 to a company controlled by Alice Huang and renamed Megaspeed, a Singapore-based data center company later involved in the Nvidia chip controversy.

Speedmatrix

Speedmatrix is a Malaysian company identified by Malaysia's trade ministry as a subsidiary of Singapore-based data center firm Megaspeed. Import records show it bought $4.13 billion worth of Nvidia AI chips and related products between May 2024 and February 2026, with the total reaching about $4.81 billion including less specific entries. Its chip purchases are under scrutiny amid alleged Nvidia chip diversion investigations.

ByteDance

According to the article, ByteDance is a heavy user of rented computing in Southeast Asia and would face stricter scrutiny under new U.S. restrictions. Its principal overseas businesses include social media and e-commerce. ByteDance has begun seeking computing capacity in North America and elsewhere to reduce its exposure to potential disruptions in Southeast Asia.

Alibaba

Alibaba is a heavy user of rented computing in Southeast Asia and faces stricter scrutiny under new U.S. export rules. CEO Eddie Wu said the rules would affect products and services planned for next year. Alibaba plans to invest heavily in its T-Head chip supply chain over two years, sourcing most additional AI chips for Alibaba Cloud from that business.

Tencent

Tencent's president Martin Lau said at a Nov. 18 earnings briefing that the next version of its Hunyuan model could take longer to release. He noted Chinese AI companies would increasingly rely on domestic chips, with new U.S. restrictions accelerating that transition.

Meta

In late October, Nvidia announced a $3 billion investment in newly issued Meta shares. Meta simultaneously announced expanded deployment of Nvidia computing and networking products, extending Nvidia's investment-and-purchasing model to another major tech company.

CoreWeave

CoreWeave became the model for Nvidia's financing arrangements in the AI boom. Nvidia began investing in CoreWeave in 2023, provided guarantees associated with leases for land, power and buildings, and agreed to purchase unsold cloud capacity. This pattern spread to other cloud providers, AI labs, and infrastructure projects.

BlackRock

According to the article, BlackRock is one of six financial institutions that partnered with Nvidia on an AI-infrastructure financing platform. The initiative was intended to initially attract more than $500 billion in outside capital, but the plan had yet to materialize.

Blackstone

Blackstone is one of six financial institutions, including BlackRock and Goldman Sachs, that partnered with Nvidia on an AI-infrastructure financing platform. The platform was initially intended to attract more than $500 billion in outside capital, though that financing plan had yet to materialize.

Goldman Sachs

According to the article, Nvidia partnered with Goldman Sachs, along with BlackRock and Blackstone, on an AI-infrastructure financing platform intended initially to attract more than $500 billion in outside capital. That financing plan had yet to materialize, according to the report.

Barclays

Barclays warned that circular transactions could inflate apparent demand and valuations when spending depended more on companies financing one another than on independent customers. This warning came amid Nvidia's growing off-balance-sheet commitments and guarantee exposure of about $550 billion, raising concerns about whether investment flows were overstating independent demand.

Morgan Stanley

Morgan Stanley said Nvidia’s near-term product pipeline limited immediate business risk, but warned that development and execution risks could rise over the next 12 to 18 months.

Citi

Citi said that weaker Nvidia control over critical supply chain resources could benefit AMD and custom chips developed by Google, Amazon and Microsoft.

AMD

AMD (超威半导体) is mentioned in the article as a potential beneficiary of Nvidia's leadership crisis. According to Citi, weaker Nvidia control over critical supply chain resources could benefit AMD and custom chips developed by Google, Amazon, and Microsoft, giving competitors an opportunity to gain ground.

Google

According to the article, Google develops custom chips that could benefit from weaker Nvidia control over critical supply chain resources, potentially gaining competitive ground alongside AMD and Amazon.

Amazon

Amazon is mentioned in the article as a developer of custom chips that could benefit from Nvidia's reduced control over critical supply chain resources, according to Citi. No further details about Amazon are provided.

Microsoft

According to the article, Microsoft is mentioned as one of the companies that could benefit from weaker Nvidia control over critical supply chain resources, alongside AMD and Google, through its development of custom chips.

Citadel Securities

Citadel Securities hosted an event in October 2025 where Jensen Huang said China has roughly half the world's AI researchers. He argued that restricting Nvidia exports would cut this large research community off from American technology, potentially accelerating development of alternatives and weakening U.S. technological dominance.

Counterpoint

Counterpoint is cited in the article regarding Nvidia's potential slower product rollout after Jensen Huang's departure. It said a slower cycle could give engineers and suppliers more time to adapt and ease research and capital spending pressure, but at the cost of slower growth.

Duan & Duan

Duan & Duan (段和段) is a law firm with a Beijing office. Its deputy director, Ding Jie, commented on Jensen Huang's resignation from Nvidia, saying it could help separate Nvidia's legal and disclosure obligations from investigations involving Huang personally, but would not shield the company from liability.

Fox News

Fox News is mentioned in the article as the outlet where President Trump gave a Nov. 19 interview in Shenzhen, during which he said he might allow sales of Nvidia's latest Blackwell chips to China in two or three years if he remained in office. This followed his earlier comments that selling less advanced chips to China was not necessarily bad.

ImportGenius

ImportGenius is a data provider that supplies import records. According to the article, its records showed that Malaysian company Speedmatrix, identified as a Megaspeed subsidiary, bought $4.13 billion worth of Nvidia AI chips and related products between May 2024 and February 2026. Including less-specific entries, the total reached about $4.81 billion.
