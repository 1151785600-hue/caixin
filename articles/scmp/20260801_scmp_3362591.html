Negeri Sembilan votes as Malaysia’s political allies lock horns again

SCMP | FREE | 1051 words | 2026-08-01

Voters in Malaysia’s Negeri Sembilan headed to the polls on Saturday, the second election in the past month as political allies governing the country locked horns at state levels.

Candidates from Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim’s Pakatan Harapan (PH) coalition are going head-to-head with those of federal partner Barisan Nasional (BN), which trounced its rival in last month’s Johor race.

Voter turnout is at 28.11 per cent as of 11am, according to the Election Commission.

The commission is targeting a voter turnout of between 72 and 75 per cent. In the 2023 polls, final voter turnout was at 68.35 per cent.

The target is higher this year as some 20,000 new voters are eligible to cast their ballots the first time – coupled with intense campaigning from contesting parties.

Negeri Sembilan has traditionally been a stronghold for PH, which won 17 seats in 2023 while BN secured 14, allowing the two coalitions to form the state government with a combined 31-seat majority.

The opposition Perikatan Nasional (PN) won the remaining five seats.

Nearly 890,000 voters are registered to cast their ballots at 401 polling centres statewide, with 36 state assembly members to be elected from a field of 103 candidates in tight three-way races.

Parties contesting have centred their campaigns on the economy, jobs and public service.

One voter, Mohd Afzanizam Abdul Rashid, 49, who cast his ballot in Paroi in the morning said he hoped that whoever leads the next state government would be highly competent and possess unquestionable integrity.

The chief economist at Bank Muamalat Malaysia said Negeri Sembilan’s economic potential and future were untapped. “The state govt can play an effective role as an enabler that facilitates a competitive and fair economy for businesses, investors and the man on the street.”

Electoral cooperation between BN and PN has also shaped the battle. While they are contesting separately, BN deputy president Mohamad Hasan has said his machinery will help PN candidates in agreed constituencies under a seat understanding, similar to the strategy adopted in the Johor election.

The Negeri Sembilan polls will further test the strength of Anwar’s coalition government and whether PH can maintain momentum despite frustrations over rising costs of living and wider economic pressures.

The Johor showdown on July 11 saw BN sweeping 48 seats – up from 40 in 2022, while PH managed only eight and lost ground from its previous 12. BN’s two-thirds majority retained gives it absolute power to pass constitutional amendments and redraw electoral boundaries in Johor.

Analysts say voter turnout, Malay sentiment and whether opposition PN can gain ground in 23 Malay-majority constituencies will determine if PH can still retain power in Negeri Sembilan.

“Voter turnout is very critical, especially in the Malay-majority constituency,” Azmi Hassan, senior fellow at the Nusantara Academy for Strategic Research, told This Week in Asia.

Early voting was held on Tuesday, with total turnout reaching 91.06 per cent or 14,995 early voters, local media reports said.

In the 2023 state election, early voters accounted for 56.67 per cent, with 14,778 of 26,079 eligible individuals casting ballots.

Azmi said BN could score support from Malay voters in constituencies where PN was not contesting.

“The cooperation between BN and PN could influence voting in Malay-majority constituencies, with PN supporters potentially backing BN candidates where PN is not contesting,” he said.

A pre-election survey released on Thursday by IDE Research Centre, a Malaysian-based think tank and research hub, suggests turnout and late-deciding voters could prove decisive.

It found that 51.9 per cent of voters aged between 18 and 30 said they were certain to vote compared with 67.4 per cent of those aged 31 to 60, with voting motivation lower among Chinese and Indians than Malays.

Pollster Ilham Centre pointed to a tighter contest this time, projecting BN and PN to secure a majority if Malay votes were consolidated.

“PH continues to face a significant deficit in Malay support, while Chennah, Linggi, Nilai, Repah and Temiang remain highly competitive and too close to call,” it said in its pre-election assessment released on Friday.

High-profile candidates include Democratic Action Party Secretary General and Transport Minister Anthony Loke, defending his Chennah seat, and Negeri Sembilan caretaker chief minister Aminuddin Harun, who is contesting in Linggi instead of his long-held Sikamat constituency.

Mohamad Hasan, who is also foreign minister, is seeking another term in Ranrau, while Negeri Sembilan Umno chief Jalaluddin Alias will defend Pertang.

The snap election was called after months of political and constitutional uncertainty, during which disputes involving the Negeri Sembilan palace coincided with the collapse of the PH-BN state administration after BN withdrew support for Aminuddin. The state legislative assembly was officially dissolved on June 5.

The crisis has put political stability under the spotlight, with PH and BN now battling for a clearer mandate to govern the state.

Reform campaign

The polls have also seen the long-delayed release of the Royal Commission of Inquiry report on the country’s Islamic pilgrimage fund, injecting governance and corruption into the final stretch of the campaign.

Analysts say while the report was justified on public interest grounds, its timing created opportunities for PH to reinforce its reform and anti-corruption narrative.

Awang Azman Awang Pawi, an associate professor at Universiti Malaya, said the report represented a governance measure and its findings could help mobilise PH’s existing Malay supporters and put BN temporarily on the defensive.

“The issue could help PH among three groups: Malay-Muslim voters, undecided voters, and younger or professionally oriented Malay voters concerned about political appointments and institutional competence,” he said.

Asrul Hadi Abdullah Sani, associate vice-president at The Asia Group, said while PH remained a favourite, support among Malay voters appeared far from secure.

“Malay voter sentiment will be central because even a modest shift could determine several Malay-majority and mixed constituencies, particularly if Chinese and Indian turnout is lower,” he said.

According to Asrul, key contests could be in marginal Malay-majority and mixed constituencies.

“A strong BN win would suggest that Johor may have marked the beginning of a broader political recovery, particularly if it performs well among Malay voters and in mixed constituencies,” he said.

“A convincing PH victory would indicate that BN’s Johor success was driven more by local strength than by a wider national shift.”

Polling centres will close at 6pm, with the Election Commission expecting full results as early as 10pm.
