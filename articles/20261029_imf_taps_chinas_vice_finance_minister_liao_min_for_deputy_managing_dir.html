IMF Taps China’s Vice Finance Minister Liao Min for Deputy Managing Director Role

Caixin Global | FULLTEXT | 663 words | 2026-10-29 04:09

1. The IMF's managing director, Kristalina Georgieva, has proposed appointing Chinese Vice Finance Minister Liao Min as a deputy managing director, succeeding Li Bo, whose term ends November 30. Liao would take office on December 1 and would be the first Chinese official to move into this role from the Ministry of Finance, as his three predecessors all joined from deputy governor positions at the People's Bank of China. Since 2011, one of the four deputy managing director posts has consistently been held by a Chinese national.

2. Liao, 57, has been vice finance minister since May 2018. Before joining the finance ministry and the Office of the Central Financial and Economic Affairs Commission, he spent years at the China Banking Regulatory Commission. His responsibilities have included economic and trade dialogue with the U.S. and Germany, later expanding to financial affairs, international economic relations, international financial cooperation and tariff departments. His appointment comes as the IMF confronts trade fragmentation, debates over the international monetary system and debt distress in developing economies.

3. Liao has been a core member of China's negotiating team through successive rounds of trade tensions with the U.S. He participated in high-level negotiations from the escalation in 2018 through the phase one agreement in January 2020. During the Biden administration, he co-led an economic working group with Jay Shambaugh, then the U.S. Treasury's undersecretary. When Treasury Secretary Janet Yellen raised overcapacity concerns in 2024, Liao argued that capacity should be assessed in the context of economic globalization and that trade protectionism would not resolve the problem.

4. After Trump began his second term in 2025 and imposed additional tariffs, Liao played a central role in talks in Geneva. Renewed negotiations operated through a U.S.-China economic and trade consultation mechanism, with He Lifeng leading the Chinese side and Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and U.S. Trade Representative Jamieson Greer representing Washington. The two sides held eight rounds of talks between May 2025 and September 2026.

5. Liao's international responsibilities have included the China-Germany High-Level Financial Dialogue and G-20 cooperation. He attended Paris Club forums and helped deepen China's engagement with the informal group of official creditors, establishing regular technical exchanges on debt restructuring and sustainability. He has overseen China's bilateral debt restructuring work for Zambia, Ghana and Ethiopia under the Common Framework and developed a close working relationship with Gita Gopinath. Sources said he earned a strong reputation within the IMF, credited to his English fluency, pragmatic approach and international perspective.

6. Born in December 1968, Liao earned degrees at Peking University and an MBA from Cambridge's Judge Business School. He began his career at China Everbright Group and Bank of China, then served in various positions at the banking regulator, headed the Shanghai banking regulatory bureau, and became director of the fourth bureau in the Central Financial and Economic Affairs Commission in 2016.

7. His finance ministry portfolio broadened since 2023 to include fiscal responsibilities. At an August 2026 briefing, he outlined fiscal plans following a Politburo call for additional measures: budgeted spending exceeding 30 trillion yuan, record new government bond issuance of 11.9 trillion yuan, and central transfers to local governments totaling 10.4 trillion yuan. Measures included special sovereign bonds for capital injections into banks, interest subsidies, and a mortgage interest subsidy program. He said coordination between fiscal and financial policies could increase the impact of public spending.

8. His earlier regulatory work focused on identifying risks before they spread. During his Shanghai tenure, the nonperforming-loan deviation ratio remained around 10%, below the national average. He published research on systemic risk and argued that regulation must act countercyclically against the financial cycle. He also studied the interaction between finance and technology, warning that valuation models for tech groups challenged conventional frameworks and that AI could disrupt financial sector jobs more broadly than earlier innovations.
