In Depth: CEO’s Affair Pulls Nvidia Deeper Into the U.S.-China Chip Fight

Caixin Global | FULLTEXT | 693 words | 2026-11-16 10:41

1. Jensen Huang publicly admitted to cheating on his wife and apologized in a statement on Nvidia’s website, saying he had hurt his family and accepted responsibility. Nvidia shares have fallen more than 20% in the past month, the board has launched an independent investigation, and the U.S. Congress has asked Huang for a closed-door briefing on Nov. 18. The controversy involves his alleged relationship with a woman who worked for a Singapore data center company that bought $4.8 billion of Nvidia chips.

Several Silicon Valley technology professionals told Caixin that Huang faces little immediate risk of losing his job because his influence over Nvidia’s operations and stock price is immense, and an affair alone is widely seen as having limited business consequences. However, if evidence emerges that national security was harmed, that assessment could change. This matters for a roughly $4 trillion company whose chips dominate AI model training and inference, and whose CEO has been a prominent advocate for maintaining access to China.

3. Huang’s authority rests on his role as founder and operator rather than on special voting control. He founded Nvidia in 1993 and led it to a 1999 IPO; he is the only company insider on the 10-person board. Nvidia has no chairman; instead, lead independent director Stephen C. Neal helps oversee management, with quarterly independent-director meetings held without the CEO. Ownership is dispersed: BlackRock holds 7.4%, Vanguard holds 7.3%, and Huang and his wife jointly hold 3.6% through trusts. His fiscal 2026 target compensation is $35.5 million, heavily tied to operating-profit and shareholder-return targets.

4. A practical check on Huang’s power is the lack of an obvious successor. No senior manager stands out; co-founder Chris Malachowsky is still an executive-team member but no longer runs day-to-day operations. Huang’s children work at Nvidia in junior product roles. Nvidia’s culture gives employees direct access to Huang, and a 2024 book characterized his unique role as a potential single point of failure, questioning who could replace him.

5. Huang’s personal admission contrasts with the family image he often shared: he met his wife at university when he was 17 and she was 19, and they have been married for 41 years. His wife has focused on philanthropy. In his apology, Huang acknowledged letting down his family and expressed hope they could heal. The comparison with Bill Gates, whose divorce did not affect Microsoft because Gates had left its executive team, highlights how different Huang’s situation is as a sitting CEO.

6. The national-security dimension makes the affair more serious. The woman identified as his suspected partner, Alice Huang (Huang Le), is about 42 and was an executive at Megaspeed, a Nvidia customer that purchased $4.8 billion in chips. A lawyer told Caixin that an affair alone would be unlikely to prompt dismissal, but export controls are crucial: the key questions are how many chips Alice Huang bought, whether any breached U.S. export restrictions, and whether Jensen Huang knew. Nvidia’s board said it places U.S. national and economic security first and currently has no reason to believe national security was compromised.

7. Nvidia’s rise began with GPUs and expanded after 2012 showed deep learning trains well on them. U.S. export controls have reduced Nvidia’s China presence: after restrictions under Joe Biden, Nvidia introduced H20 chips for China in early 2024, and President Donald Trump’s administration required licenses for H20 exports in April 2025. After backdoor allegations and regulatory pressure, Washington in January 2026 approved H200 exports to China subject to a 25% revenue-sharing requirement; Huang then announced production had restarted. Huang has criticized the controls, saying Nvidia’s Chinese market share was zero and that abandoning the entire market made no strategic sense. He visited China in 2024 and 2025, met Vice Premier He Lifeng, and led an open letter opposing sanctions on Chinese open-source model developers. With Democrats set to control Congress next year, he may face greater regulatory pressure despite his alignment with Republican-supported lighter AI regulation.
