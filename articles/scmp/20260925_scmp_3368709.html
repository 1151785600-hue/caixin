Beyond the Rose Garden and the military review, Trump and Xi get down to business

SCMP | FREE | 1370 words | 2026-09-25

After weeks of frustration, delays and posturing, Chinese President Xi Jinping and US President Donald Trump met on Thursday, holding talks on several bilateral irritants and announcing an ill-defined “new joint arrangement” as they kicked off the most consequential day of their high-stakes summit.

The arrangement was “good news for businesses, ordinary people and the global economy”, Xi said, according to state news agency Xinhua, without elaborating. “We must turn this vision into action,” he added.

Earlier in the day, Trump suggested that the two sides made progress on a Board of Trade mechanism aimed at reducing tariffs on non-strategic products in equal measure.

At Thursday’s state dinner, Trump gave Xi a large sculpture of a bald eagle, the US national symbol, adding that he and Xi represent different political systems but “the ties between our people endure, and we’ve never gotten along better”.

“Together we can continue to build a relationship that promotes prosperity and security for future generations,” he said.

Xi, in his second speech of the day, followed in offering a toast to the US president, adding that this was the second state visit between the two leaders in a year “making history”.

“This has added new substance to the constructive China-US relationship of strategic stability and provided new strategic guidance for China-US relations.”

Both leaders also reaffirmed support for the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation and G20 groupings, which have summits in November in Shenzhen and in December in Miami respectively, teasing two more possible meetings this year.

The leaders started the morning with a formal arrival ceremony on the White House South Lawn and Rose Garden that included nearly 500 military personnel, the Chinese and American national anthems and opening remarks from the presidents.

Both leaders adopted a notably rosy tone throughout the day, highlighting areas of common ground, cultural touchstones and partnership efforts during World War II.

There was much the two nations could achieve, Trump said, citing people-to-people connections that “even made us allies in World War II”.

Trump added that security, technology and “superintelligence”, a term he has offered up to replace artificial intelligence, were key topics of discussion between the two sides.

“The decisions we make in these areas today can promote peace and prosperity for decades and decades to come,” he said.

Xi followed in a similar vein, inviting 100,000 young Americans to visit China, offering a pair of giant pandas to Zoo Atlanta and calling for greater cooperation.

“I am ready to work with you to steer the giant ship of the China-US relationship on a steady course towards the future,” Xi said in front of US and Chinese flags.

“China and the United States are different in national conditions,” added Xi during his first summit on US soil since 2015. “But through candid, in-depth and continued dialogue, our two countries can understand each other better.”

Xi gave a nod to Trump’s “America first” catchphrase twice during the day even as he emphasised the importance of recognising China as an equal, underscoring the messaging by both leaders to their respective domestic audiences.

“Achieving the great rejuvenation of the Chinese nation and making America great again can go hand in hand,” he said.

“Let us keep advancing the cause of friendship between our two peoples and work together to build a better world.”

Their comments were followed by a military review in the Rose Garden, part of which Trump has cemented over, capped by a flyover of military aircraft before the pair retired for bilateral meetings behind closed doors.

Their time without their respective retinues lasted less than an hour. Xi stressed Beijing’s position on Taiwan, urging Trump to “carefully” consider the US’ stance and oppose Taiwan independence, according to Chinese state media.

China’s first lady, Peng Liyuan, and US first lady Melania Trump also attended the welcome ceremony, attracting much attention during the event before they broke off to tour the National Museum of Asian Art.

Beneath the day’s florid language and repeated statements of friendship are a host of critical issues.

Analysts expect few breakthroughs or substantive deliverables during the summit, which ends on Friday, involving such difficult issues as artificial intelligence safeguards, rare earth minerals, trade tariffs, Taiwan arms sales, the Iran war, Ukraine and bilateral military relations.

“This is going to be a summit that’s relatively underwhelming with a small number of deliverables, but I think that works mainly to China’s advantage,” said Scott Kennedy, senior adviser at the Centre for Strategic and International Studies, given the lack of movement on overcapacity or other structural issues.

Relative to last year’s escalating tariffs and harsh rhetoric, however, many welcomed signs of a floor, however wobbly, under the fraught relationship.

One deliverable of sorts emerged hours before Xi’s aircraft even touched down when US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent announced that the two sides would extend a trade truce reached nearly a year ago in South Korea, but only for two months.

In far loftier and substantive comments than his US counterpart, Xi touched on military communication, fentanyl, AI, investment and the need for more communication.

The nations’ militaries could “maintain regular dialogue” as part of an overall relationship that involved “healthy” competition that was “kept within bounds”, he said.

“We have both the capability and responsibility to develop and manage AI for good and ensure that the development of AI is always under human control,” the Chinese leader added. “We hope Chinese companies are treated fairly here in the United States.”

Xi’s visit drew local supporters outside the White House, many singing the Chinese song “Bless the Motherland”, as well as pro-democracy protesters, including a parade of Tibetan protesters that marched from the Chinese embassy.

Thursday’s talks are the heart of the three-day trip and followed Trump’s unusual move of greeting Xi in person at the foot of the jetway on Wednesday at Joint Base Andrews after a 15-hour flight, an expression of Trump’s respect for, and desire for respect from, the Chinese leader.

But the world has come to expect the unexpected from Trump, a proud disrupter who relishes slamming through norms and flexing his power and US military might.

Planners organised the summit to mirror Trump’s summit in Beijing four months ago. The time on the ground will be limited, with no side visits outside the capital and Thursday’s state dinner. The reciprocity was meant to underscore respect and the equality of the two wary competitors, an issue of particular importance to Beijing.

While the White House has been juggling its Iran war of choice, a Canadian trade showdown, Trump’s record-low popularity and pending November elections, summit preparations have been frustrated by the small number of US decision-makers and an impetuous president inclined to rip up plans on short notice, according to sources.

With low expectations for deliverables this week, some movement on artificial intelligence is high on the agenda. The issue has been a source of rising tensions in recent months as the two nations tout competing visions, promote rival national champions and oversee different regulatory environments.

Another entrenched problem, one of particular concern to the US side, is Iran. Trump badly wants Beijing’s help in forcing Tehran to the bargaining table to end a war he started over six months ago.

But China, which had been buying more than 80 per cent of Iran’s exported crude before the conflict, has baulked at increasing the pressure on its Middle Eastern partner, although it has stopped short of supporting Tehran in obvious military ways.

Another key issue involves the 90 per cent of global rare earth mineral processing that China controls. In recent months, Beijing has dispensed these critical resources sparingly to US companies.

China unsheathed its highly effective cudgel after “Liberation Day”, when Trump threatened tariffs of over 80 per cent on the Asian export giant, affording China enormous leverage that it is in no hurry to give up.

“China-US cooperation may not solve every problem in the world,” Xi said on Thursday. “But without our cooperation, it would be hard to solve many of the world’s problems.”
