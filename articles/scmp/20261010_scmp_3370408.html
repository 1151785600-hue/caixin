A fortune, a HK$72m flat and a family feud: inside the Abby Choi murder trial

SCMP | FREE | 1039 words | 2026-10-10

A bitter family dispute over the proposed sale of a HK$72 million flat allegedly led a retired police officer and his two sons to murder socialite Abby Choi Tin-fung three years ago, a Hong Kong court has heard.

The trial over the 28-year-old model’s killing, which shocked Hong Kong and attracted international attention, started after a one-week delay caused by unresolved preliminary issues and jury selection.

Choi’s mother, Cheung Yin-fa, told the High Court how her relationship with her daughter’s former in-laws deteriorated, accusing them of clinging to her family’s fortune and having “bitten the hand that fed them”.

Giving evidence for the prosecution, Cheung became emotional at times as she accused Choi’s former in-laws of living in luxury without contributing to the upbringing of the two children her daughter had with her ex-husband.

She wept in the witness box while watching footage of her daughter welcoming guests to the luxury flat on February 21, 2023, the day she was last seen alive.

“My daughter was a really good daughter, a really filial daughter,” Cheung said tearfully. “She trusted and gave her kindness to the wrong people. This family really bit the hand that fed them.”

Choi’s ex-husband, Alex Kwong Kong-chi, 31, his father, Kwong Kau, 68, and elder brother, Anthony Kwong Kong-kit, 35, are standing trial after pleading not guilty to murdering her.

Kwong Kau, a retired police sergeant, has also denied preventing the lawful burial of Choi’s body. His two sons pleaded guilty to that offence before the trial.

The killing came to light after some of Choi’s dismembered remains were discovered in a 600 square foot flat in Tai Po’s Lung Mei Village on February 24, 2023.

Inside, police found a hammer – the alleged murder weapon – a meat mincer, two cooking pots containing Choi’s remains, and multiple saws and knives.

The prosecution alleges that the killing stemmed from a family feud over the proposed sale of a HK$72 million flat at the Kadooria residential complex in Ho Man Tin. Kwong Kau, the property’s registered owner, allegedly resented Choi’s attempts to sell it and enlisted his sons to help kill her so he could keep it.

Prosecutors have described the alleged murder as a carefully crafted plot in which each defendant played a role. It allegedly involved renting the village flat, driving Choi to the remote location on the pretext of collecting her daughter from school, and striking her with a hammer during the journey.

Prosecutor on fiat Charlotte Draycott SC suggested that money was the motive and highlighted the significant wealth gap between the two families.

Choi’s stepfather, Choi Cho-san, was described as a wealthy businessman who provided a monthly allowance of more than HK$1 million to support his wife, Cheung, Choi and the model’s two stepsisters in Hong Kong.

The court heard that Choi received a HK$41 million flat as a wedding gift from the father of her partner, Chris Tam Fong-chun, who also came from a wealthy family, although the couple never formally registered their marriage. She was also said to have received more than HK$300 million from the Tam family over the years.

The Kwong family, according to the prosecution, was far less affluent. Kwong Kau’s wife, Jenny Li Sui-heung, and their two sons were said to be unemployed, while he relied on an annual pension of HK$145,000 after retiring from the police force in 2006.

The court also heard that Kwong Kau had worked as a taxi driver, although his lawyer disputed this.

Cheung testified that her daughter had registered the Kadooria flat in Kwong Kau’s name to avoid HK$9 million in double stamp duty, with the understanding that he would hold it on trust for the two children she had with Alex Kwong.

But when Choi indicated in June 2022 that she wanted to sell the flat and move the children closer to their school in Tuen Mun, Kwong Kau allegedly attacked her and threatened to kill her and her entire family if she went ahead.

In a series of audio messages to her mother in October and November 2022, Choi complained that her former in-laws were ungrateful despite her having supported their lavish lifestyle for years. She also expressed regret at having registered a property in Hainan province and a Rolls-Royce in her former father-in-law’s name.

Cheung said she and her daughter had supported the Kwong family for years but received nothing in return.

She told the court that Alex Kwong had obtained more than HK$70 million from his ex-wife after saying he would invest the money in luxury watches. He also persuaded Choi to lend him her credit card to pay his living expenses in mainland China while he was evading justice over his involvement in a HK$6.3 million theft case, Cheung said.

Anthony Kwong received HK$2 million from Choi to run an egg waffle shop in Sham Shui Po, according to Cheung. He became Choi’s stand-in chauffeur in early 2023 after he and his mother repeatedly urged her to give him a job, the witness added.

The court also heard that Choi gave Kwong Kau a monthly allowance of HK$20,000 and paid more than HK$200,000 towards his medical expenses when he was seriously ill.

During cross-examination, defence lawyers challenged Cheung’s account of their clients’ financial dependence on Choi.

Anthony Kwong’s lawyer, Trevor Beel, said his client had never received substantial financial support from Choi. He said the egg waffle shop had been set up at her request because her two children loved the snack.

Oliver Davies, representing Kwong Kau, described his client as a businessman with considerable savings who had contributed half of the Kadooria flat’s HK$60 million purchase price in 2019.

He challenged Cheung’s account of her husband’s wealth, arguing that Choi Cho-san was “constantly in debt” and could not have afforded to give her HK$1 million a month.

Davies also pointed to the limited assets Choi left behind, suggesting that she was not particularly wealthy either.

Cheung will face further cross-examination by Alex Kwong’s counsel, Andrew Raffell, when the trial resumes on Monday.
