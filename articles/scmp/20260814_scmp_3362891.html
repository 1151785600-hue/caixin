Vintage charm is blended with modern elegance in a timeless Hong Kong flat

SCMP | FREE | 1032 words | 2026-08-14

Needing a designer to renovate his newly purchased, 38-year-old apartment in Central, businessman Harry Sun found the perfect fit in an unexpected place. He and interior designer Dominic Cheung, co-founder of Crimson Company, were both regulars at the same tailor’s shop – and when their paths crossed, the introduction sparked an instant connection. The two shared sensibilities that extended beyond sartorial savoir faire to a mutual appreciation for classicism with a youthful touch.

Thus began a harmonious collaboration to make over the 620 sq ft mid-rise apartment in quintessentially “old” Hong Kong, with its views of historic St Paul’s Church ringed by mature trees.

Sating his penchant for vintage and antique European designs, Sun already had an assemblage of finds he’d been collecting from markets since his university days in Britain. These would inform the interiors, as Cheung revised the floor plan from two bedrooms to one, enabling a bigger dining room and a slightly enlarged bathroom.

“Our goal was to create a space with a strong, yet quiet, architectural identity that feels both intentional and deeply personal,” says Cheung. “By integrating classic European details within a modern, clean envelope, we created a backdrop designed not to dominate, but to age gracefully over the years.”

His client’s brief for “cosiness” – a space that could tastefully house his furniture and objets d’art – excited Cheung, himself a lover of things vintage.

“Vintage pieces big and small can introduce a storytelling element, adding more depth to the space. Our shared vision was to juxtapose old and new, without making it look like an antique shop.”

An 18th century Louis XVI commode and a 19th century Louis Philippe gilded mirror set the tone for the dining room, where, additionally, mid-century modern Wishbone chairs are paired with a custom-made Calacatta marble table. Resisting the temptation to install ornate chandeliers, lighting designer and Crimson Company co-founder Rosana Asencios opted instead for two simple brass pendants. “Their patina will change with age,” she says, “responding very well to the antique pieces and the history in the room. It’s about having a sleek accent, without being too overwhelming.”

French-inspired wall mouldings and herringbone oak flooring lend subtle sophistication.

In the separate living room, twin bay windows frame neighbourhood views, and a mahogany library wall houses Sun’s collections of books – history and literature among his favourites – and vinyl records.

A green velvet sofa adds a youthful, mid-century modernist touch beside a vintage handmade rug. Sun’s prized analogue turntable sits atop a minimalist, Scandinavian-inspired coffee table; all other audiovisual equipment is concealed behind slatted joinery inlaid within the wall panelling.

With his client needing to work from home only occasionally, Cheng tucked a study nook into a walkway, behind two pocket doors inlaid with antique glass panels.

Cheung’s intent to “frame different moments in the home” is evident in the simply furnished bedroom, where two elegant oak and glass pendants, a custom bed and an oak-finish wardrobe take centre stage. The only hung artwork is a small framed print picked up on a trip to Italy during his school days.

Proving that even a compact kitchen can have style as well as substance, handmade Moroccan splashback tiles pair gracefully with natural stone benchtops, brass knobs and pulls punctuating the light-grey, powder-finished cabinetry.

The same quiet refinement carries through to the bathroom. Neither space is merely entered – one arrives from the dining room via a dramatic Nero Marquina marble arched portal and floor threshold.

Every corner and every wall holds a focal point, yet nothing feels forced or crowded.

“In this not-so-big apartment, a lot is happening, but you don’t feel like a lot is happening. The flow is easy,” Cheung says.

The result is a harmonious dialogue between modernism and classicism, where all details are intentional but never laboured.

A delighted Sun says the refurbishment, completed in December 2024, never ceases to amaze him. “It’s one of those places that’s well built, well designed and feels very comfortable when you live there.”

Living room

Mahogany bookcase, designed by Crimson Company and built by Design for Life. Sofa, from B&B Italia. Coffee table, from Norr11. Record turntable, from Cambridge Audio. Side table, from Mater. Wall lamp, from Santa & Cole. Vintage handmade rug, from Blanc Marine Boutique. Artwork is a Liu Peng landscape, from Asian Art Platform Singapore

Dining room

Calacatta Viola dining marble, designed by Crimson Company and built by Design for Life. Wishbone chairs, from Carl Hansen & Søn. Candela di Vals pendant lamps in natural brass, from Viabizzuno. Sun found the small oil painting of a fig years ago. In the alcove, a Louis XVI mahogany commode with Carrara marble top, from Inessa Stewart’s Antiques. Above it, a Louis Philippe gilt mirror with crown, from 1stDibs. Behind the mirror, Ianthe Mono wallpaper in Lapis from Liberty Fabrics

Dining room detail

Nero Marquina marble arched portal defines the threshold leading to the bathroom and kitchen. Wall mouldings and wall finish, in St Leo plaster paint (stleointeriors.com). Polished brass dimmer switches and sockets, from Varilight (varilight.co.uk). Wall-hung printed abstract, from The Poster Club (theposterclub.com)

Study nook

Pocket doors in the study nook feature antique glass inlay panels. Vintage brass handles from Etsy. Desk joinery in burl wood veneer, designed by Crimson Company and built by Design for Life, who also installed the herringbone-pattern engineered timber floor

Bathroom

Brass-framed mirror and cabinetry with custom Calacatta Viola marble vanity top, designed by Crimson Company and built by Design for Life. Basin and French gold-finish tapware, from Kohler. Small lamp, from Audo Copenhagen. Overhead light, from Visual Comfort

Bedroom

Bed with upholstered headboard, custom-designed by Crimson Company and built by Sankon Interiors. Pair of hanging lamps, from Santa & Cole. Bedside tables, from Ikea. Wardrobe in oak timber finish, designed by Crimson Company and built by Design for Life

Kitchen

Kitchen cabinetry with natural quartz stone tops, by Crimson Company and Design for Life. Handmade Zellige tiles, from Zia Tile. Brass knobs and pulls, from Willow & Stone. Artwork on benchtop, by Molly Supplee
