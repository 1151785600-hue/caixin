Malaysia’s Najib to remain in prison as legal questions linger despite fine payment

SCMP | FREE | 1080 words | 2026-11-07

Malaysia’s disgraced former prime minister Najib Razak moved closer to house arrest after Umno on Friday paid a 50 million ringgit (US$12.3 million) fine required under the terms of his royal pardon.

The clemency granted by King Sultan Ibrahim Iskandar in September allows Najib to serve the remainder of his sentence under house arrest until August 2028, after more than four years behind bars for corruption linked to the 1MDB sovereign wealth fund.

Despite the payment, Najib has not been transferred home yet. The Attorney General’s Chambers, in a statement issued later on Friday, said Najib should remain in prison until legal questions over his royal pardon and its implications for his separate 1MDB sentence were resolved.

His exit from prison may renew scrutiny of Prime Minister Anwar Ibrahim’s governing alliances, analysts said, after Transport Minister Anthony Loke from the Democratic Action Party (DAP) pledged to resign once Najib begins his house arrest.

Loke reiterated on Friday that his resignation would take effect immediately upon Najib’s arrival home, but said Home Minister Saifuddin Nasution Ismail had told him the former prime minister had not yet been transferred to house arrest.

“I received confirmation from Saifuddin that Najib was taken to a dental clinic for a dental examination scheduled for today,” Loke told reporters, addressing reports that Najib had left Kajang Prison. “That means he has not yet been sent home.”

He added: “If he steps into his house tonight, it takes effect tonight. If tomorrow, then tomorrow.”

Umno secretary general Asyraf Wajdi Dusuki said the Najib Razak Solidarity Fund, launched on September 18, had raised sufficient money and the party had completed the payment process at the Kuala Lumpur High Court on Friday morning.

“The amount collected through the Najib Razak Solidarity Fund, which was launched on September 18 to help former prime minister Najib Razak, has reached its target and is sufficient,” he said in a statement. “Umno completed all payment procedures at the Kuala Lumpur High Court this morning.”

However, the statement did not disclose the total amount raised or identify major donors.

“Umno hereby officially closes the special Najib Razak Solidarity Fund account established to receive public donations and there is no further need for crowdfunding for this purpose,” Asyraf said.

Najib’s lawyer, Muhammad Farhan, told reporters that the court-issued house arrest order was delivered to Kajang Prison. Farhan and Najib’s special officer Ahmad Lutfi Azhar were also spotted entering the court’s criminal registry office in the afternoon.

Local media reported that police tightened security around Najib’s residence in Kuala Lumpur’s upmarket Langgak Duta neighbourhood. His lawyer previously identified the home as the designated location in the house arrest documents.

Anwar said he differed with Umno over its payment of Najib’s fine.

“My personal view is that I disagree because, although we have a sense of friendship with Najib, that should not give the public the impression that corruption involving billions of ringgit can be tolerated in this way,” he told reporters after Friday prayers.

“Umno’s decision is its right and we respect the legal process as well as the decision made by the king.”

Amir Fareed Rahim, director of strategy at political consultancy KRA Group, told This Week in Asia that Najib’s house arrest would embolden Umno, energise the party’s members and strengthen its confidence in reclaiming conservative Malay votes.

“A revitalised Umno may believe it can regain lost ground on its own rather than rely on an electoral partnership with PAS,” he said.

Umno’s Barisan Nasional coalition and Pan-Malaysian Islamic Party have agreed to cooperate in the November 14 Melaka state election, including joint campaigning and a shared manifesto, despite Umno remaining part of Anwar’s federal government.

Amir cautioned that younger and undecided voters could instead be reminded of the corruption scandals surrounding Umno, adding that Najib’s opponents could punish Anwar’s Pakatan Harapan coalition if they blamed it for the concession.

That discontent could undermine turnout among PH supporters, although a rallying of the coalition base was also possible, he said.

Ahmad Mohsein Azman, a senior analyst at consultancy BowerGroupAsia, said the immediate political test would be whether Loke followed through on his resignation pledge and how Anwar managed the repercussions.

Loke, who leads DAP, resigned from cabinet in September. He said it would take effect once Najib returned home after Anwar asked him to continue serving temporarily.

Loke submitted his resignation letter in September, but Anwar asked him to continue serving until the fine was paid and Najib was allowed home, the transport minister told Friday’s press conference.

“My position has not changed,” he said. “I have informed the secretary general and ministry officials that as soon as Najib arrives home, my resignation takes effect immediately.”

He said he chaired a ministry meeting after Friday’s cabinet session to settle administrative matters and ensure a smooth handover, adding that his decision reflected the party’s position.

DAP respected the king’s constitutional authority over pardons but believed granting Najib house arrest risked sending “an inappropriate message to future generations”, Loke said.

According to Mohsein, questions will be raised about how viable a coalition PH will be in the next general election.

“The best way for Anwar to regain the trust of his supporters is to move on from this issue and revitalise his broader commitment to reform; something that should be manifested even before the next general election.”

Najib, 73, began serving his sentence in August 2022 after Malaysia’s highest court upheld his conviction over the misappropriation of 42 million ringgit from SRC International, a former subsidiary of 1MDB.

His original 12-year prison term was halved in 2024 and his fine reduced from 210 million ringgit to 50 million ringgit before King Sultan Ibrahim granted the conditional house arrest pardon on September 18.

Najib, who governed Malaysia from 2009–18, has consistently denied wrongdoing over the scandal, in which more than US$1 billion allegedly flowed into accounts linked to him.

According to US investigators, more than US$4.5 billion was siphoned from 1MDB between 2009 and 2015 through an international embezzlement and money laundering scheme, financing luxury properties, a superyacht and other extravagant purchases.

The house arrest arrangement covers his SRC sentence but Najib also faces a separate 15-year prison term following his conviction in the main 1MDB case last December, scheduled to begin after the SRC term ends on August 23, 2028. The High Court last month rejected his application to stay the execution of his sentence pending appeal.
