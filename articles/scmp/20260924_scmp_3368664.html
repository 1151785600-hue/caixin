Summits for all diplomatic seasons: how the US has set the stage for Xi Jinping

SCMP | FREE | 1283 words | 2026-09-24

Chinese President Xi Jinping’s visit to the US is his first to the country since 2023 and his eighth face-to-face meeting with Donald Trump. From informal walk-and-talks to a state dinner, Xi’s meetings across four American administrations show how personal rapport and official diplomacy reflect the growing complexity of US-China relations.

Xi’s first US visit as president: a shirtsleeves summit

In June 2013, just months after assuming the presidency, Xi travelled to the United States following a tour of Latin America.

According to then US president Barack Obama, “Our decision to meet so early [in Xi’s term] signifies the importance of the US-China relationship.”

Instead of heading to the diplomatic protocol-heavy capital of Washington, Xi’s historic first meeting with Obama took place at the Annenberg Retreat at Sunnylands, a 200-acre (81-hectare) estate in Rancho Mirage, California.

Originally built as a winter haven by publishers and philanthropists Walter and Leonore Annenberg – close friends of former US president Dwight Eisenhower – the estate holds a unique place in American history as a secluded sanctuary that has often hosted political heavyweights.

Its rare blend of luxury and isolation made Sunnylands an ideal setting for the summit. Without the rigid and pre-scripted constraints of traditional diplomacy, its selection was considered unusual.

But some suspicions remained. Xi and his delegation opted to stay at the nearby Hyatt Regency in Indian Wells rather than at Sunnylands, a move many in the American media attributed to concerns about possible US eavesdropping.

Over June 7 and 8, Xi and Obama engaged in more than eight hours of dialogue spanning two formal sessions, a private dinner and a morning walk, during which they took a break in the shade on a custom California redwood bench that Obama presented to Xi.

During the dinner, Xi reportedly toasted Obama with Moutai, China’s famed high-end liquor.

China said the summit established the groundwork for a “new model of major-country relations”.

White House officials later said the meetings built a stronger relationship between the two leaders, while yielding progress on important global challenges like cybersecurity, North Korea and greenhouse gas emissions.

Xi’s first US state visit: a packed ‘unforgettable journey’

In September 2015, Xi embarked on his first formal state visit to the US. His cross-country itinerary began in Seattle, where he met with senior American officials and business leaders, continued to Washington for formal diplomatic engagements with Obama, and concluded in New York to attend the 70th anniversary summit of the United Nations.

Upon their arrival in Washington on September 24, Xi and his wife Peng Liyuan were greeted on the tarmac by then US vice-president Joe Biden and his wife Jill Biden. That evening, Obama hosted Xi for a private dinner at Blair House.

Located across Pennsylvania Avenue from the White House, Blair House serves as the US president’s guest house for visiting diplomats and dignitaries. It is also where the US president-elect and first family live before taking the oath of office.

The next morning, official protocol commenced on the White House South Lawn with a full military arrival ceremony, featuring a traditional 21-gun salute. Following a joint press conference, Xi attended a diplomatic lunch hosted in the US State Department’s Benjamin Franklin State Dining Room.

The diplomatic high point came that evening with a lavish state dinner in the White House East Room. The guest list gathered about 200 prominent figures from Hollywood, Silicon Valley, international diplomacy and corporate leadership.

The menu, created by Chinese-American Anita Lo, highlighted American cuisine accented with Chinese nuances. Guests were served wild mushroom soup, poached Maine lobster, grilled cannon of Colorado lamb and a poppy seed bread-and-butter pudding.

During the toasts, Obama acknowledged lingering strategic differences while expressing hope that the two countries’ peoples would “work together like fingers on the same hand in friendship and in peace”.

Xi described the visit as an “unforgettable journey”, noting the warmth he encountered from the west coast to the east.

Following the state dinner, American R&B artist Ne-Yo delivered a performance of his hit “Because of You”, drawing applause across the room.

Mar-a-Lago summit: ‘family-style’ diplomacy

In April 2017, Xi met with Donald Trump, early in his first presidential term, at Trump’s Mar-a-Lago resort in Florida, as both countries sought to steady a relationship frayed by months of friction.

Trump had spent his presidential campaign repeatedly hammering Beijing over trade practices and had further unsettled mainland Chinese leadership by accepting a congratulatory phone call from then Taiwanese leader Tsai Ing-wen, marking a sharp departure from decades of diplomatic precedent set in 1979.

The choice of Mar-a-Lago over a rigid White House visit was designed to create an informal backdrop for the two leaders to build personal rapport, echoing Xi’s 2013 Sunnylands summit with Obama.

As with the 2013 visit, Xi and the Chinese delegation did not stay on the grounds of the meeting venue. Instead, they checked in at the nearby Eau Palm Beach Resort & Spa.

The summit emphasised personal, family-centric diplomacy. Trump and his wife Melania Trump hosted Xi and Peng for a welcome dinner featuring pan-seared Dover sole, New York strip steak and Sonoma Chardonnay.

A stand-out moment of soft diplomacy came when Trump’s granddaughter, Arabella Kushner, who learned Mandarin from a Chinese nanny, sang the traditional folk song “Jasmine Flower” and recited classical Chinese poetry for the visiting couple.

However, the relaxed atmosphere was soon eclipsed by hard-power dynamics.

Over chocolate cake for dessert, Trump informed Xi that the US had just launched missiles against a Syrian airbase in retaliation for a chemical weapons attack. Trump later claimed Xi understood the move, but analysts said that Beijing rarely welcomed such unscripted surprises.

Filoli Estate summit: nostalgia to thaw a deep freeze?

In November 2023, Xi travelled to San Francisco to attend the 30th Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (Apec) economic leaders’ meeting and hold a summit with then US president Joe Biden. While Xi stayed at the St Regis Hotel near downtown San Francisco, the retreat itself was staged 48km (30 miles) south at the historic Filoli Estate, a secluded country house.

The summit came at a critical inflection point. Relations had plummeted to their lowest point in decades after Beijing severed high-level military contacts following then-US House speaker Nancy Pelosi’s visit to Taiwan in August 2022.

Tensions spiralled further in February 2023 when the US shot down a suspected Chinese surveillance balloon off the US east coast.

Against this backdrop, carefully choreographed personal optics were deployed to lower the temperature.

As Xi arrived at Filoli, Biden greeted him. Later, Biden pulled out a phone to show the Chinese leader a photograph of a young Xi posing in front of the Golden Gate Bridge during a 1985 trip as a county-level official.

“Do you know this young man?” Biden asked.

“I know,” Xi replied with a smile. “It’s me 38 years ago.”

“You haven’t changed a bit,” Biden quipped, drawing laughter from the crowd of staff and fellow officials.

Following a four-hour working session and lunch, the two leaders took a short walk together through the garden grounds.

Yet, tensions resurfaced shortly after the retreat concluded.

At a solo press conference, Biden repeated his view that Xi was effectively a dictator in the sense that “he’s a guy who runs a country that is a communist country that’s based on a form of government totally different than ours”.

The comment was captured alongside video of then-US secretary of state Antony Blinken visibly wincing in the audience, a moment that went viral worldwide.
