China’s Youth Unemployment Hits Record High

Caixin Global | FULLTEXT | 508 words | 2026-09-18 10:38

1. China’s surveyed urban youth unemployment rate rose to 18.9% in August, matching the highest level since the current methodology was adopted in 2024.

]

According to the National Bureau of Statistics (NBS), the rate for people aged 16 to 24, excluding students, climbed by 1 percentage point from July, marking a significant month-on-month increase and reaching a record-high under the existing statistical framework.

]

The accompanying chart from NBS and Caixin, titled "China Youth Unemployment Hits Record," visually confirms this spike, showing the rate surging to 18.9% in August after a noticeable jump from July, underscoring the severe pressure on young jobseekers in urban centers.

[para.

]

2. Unemployment among those aged 25 to 29 also rose, increasing by 0.3 percentage points to 7.5%, while the rate for workers aged 30 to 59 held steady at 3.9%.

[para.

4

]

Both age-group rates exclude students, providing a clearer view of the labor market for non-student workers. In a broader measure, the overall surveyed urban unemployment rate edged up by 0.1 percentage points to 5.3%, reflecting a modest but real deterioration in the national employment situation across all urban workers.

[para.

5

]

3. The NBS attributed the monthly increase in overall urban unemployment primarily to the seasonal influx of new graduates entering the labor market.

[para.

6

]

Youth unemployment typically peaks in August as millions of graduates complete their studies and begin actively searching for jobs, overwhelming the available opportunities during this period. The NBS anticipates that the rate will decline naturally as employment pressure eases in the following months, suggesting that this spike is largely a seasonal phenomenon rather than a permanent structural shift. However, the record-high figure also signals persistent challenges in absorbing young talent into the workforce.

4. The current youth unemployment measure deliberately excludes students, a methodology adopted in 2024.

[para.

7

]

Under the previous approach, students were included, which distorted the data since many young people are enrolled in school rather than seeking work. The NBS asserts that the revised method better reflects employment conditions among young people who have formally entered the workforce, offering a more accurate assessment of the labor market's ability to absorb new entrants. This improved measurement is intended to help inform targeted employment policies, allowing policymakers to identify vulnerable groups and design effective interventions to alleviate youth joblessness.

5. The release of this data, sourced from the NBS and reported by Caixin, highlights a critical socio-economic issue in China. While the August spike is influenced by the graduation season, the fact that the rate has reached an all-time high under the current methodology raises concerns about the structural mismatch between the supply of young labor and available job opportunities. The seasonal easing expected by the NBS may provide temporary relief, but long-term solutions will require structural reforms in education, vocational training, and industrial policy to better align young workers' skills with market demand. As the government continues to refine its data collection and policy responses, the record youth unemployment rate serves as a stark reminder of the formidable task ahead in stabilizing the job market for the nation's youth.
