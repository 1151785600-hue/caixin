In Depth: Shooting Coach’s Appeal Asks if China’s Gun Controls Go Too Far

Caixin Global | FULLTEXT | 457 words | 2026-11-23 11:51

1. A Chinese shooting coach, Tian Hong, sentenced to 10 years in prison for smuggling gun parts, is appealing her conviction. The appeal, heard Nov. 12 at the Beijing High People’s Court, centers on whether importing components like air cylinders for competition firearms constitutes weapons smuggling. Her lawyers challenged both procurement restrictions and a legal formula treating every 30 gun parts as a complete firearm.

2. Tian, born in 1966, was a competitive shooter who broke a national record in 1987. She became a coach and founded Pilin Sports in 2014 with her son Fu Yihan. In Dec. 2023, customs discovered the company suspected of importing firearm components under false pretenses. The court found they bought components from Germany and Switzerland from 2015-2023 without authorization, counting 2,446 components, including 273 for gunpowder firearms. The company was fined 3 million yuan, and Tian was sentenced to 10 years; her son got six.

3. The only designated supplier for sporting firearms in China is Huaxing Rongyao. Tian argued that provincial teams asked her company to help replace worn-out parts because procurement through Huaxing was slow and could disrupt training. She claimed selling air gun components was not meant to be profitable, noting they accounted for only 3.6% of sales and that she even replaced cylinders free of charge. Customs investigators, however, said she bypassed authorized channels, evading supervision.

Defense lawyers presented statements from coaches in various provinces confirming that Huaxing accepted orders only once a year, lacked pictures for many parts, and had long delays—some orders placed in 2024 had not arrived by Jan. 2026. Tian said more than 90% of national team coaches bought parts from her. Her lawyer argued that the 30-part conversion should not be applied mechanically when components cannot form a complete firearm, and that athletes routinely buy parts overseas themselves.

5. Chinese law and a 2014 judicial interpretation set penalties for weapons smuggling based on quantity, with 30 components counting as one firearm. However, a 2018 guidance for low-power compressed-air firearms instructed courts to look beyond quantity, considering factors like intent, procurement channels, and public danger. The defense argued that Tian's actions served legitimate sports needs and posed no threat to public safety.

6. The trial court accepted that the components circulated only in legitimate sporting activities, permitting below-statutory punishment. At the appeal, prosecutors acknowledged a distinction between Tian's supplies and weapons smuggled for sale, urging comprehensive harm assessment. Investigators concluded that buyers' procurement violations were minor and not criminal. Tian expressed no regret for helping teams but said the sentence of 10 years for her and six for her son was "far too heavy." No ruling has been issued.
