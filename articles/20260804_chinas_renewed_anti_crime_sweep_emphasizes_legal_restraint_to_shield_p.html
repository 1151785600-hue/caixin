China’s Renewed Anti-Crime Sweep Emphasizes Legal Restraint to Shield Private Sector

Caixin Global | FULLTEXT | 509 words | 2026-08-04 02:06

1. China has launched a sweeping new one-year crackdown on organized crime, beginning in July 2024, aiming to stamp out entrenched local syndicates and digital extortion rings. Authorities pledge to avoid the judicial overreach of past campaigns that ensnared private businesses. This marks the first major anti-gang initiative since the 20th National Congress.

2. The previous three-year sweep (2018–2020) dismantled 3,584 organized crime syndicates—equal to the preceding decade's total. During that campaign, 81,400 officials were investigated for protecting gangs, 226,495 individuals were convicted, and 137.37 billion yuan ($20.34 billion) in illicit assets was seized. Sustained pressure since then has drastically reduced crime, with national criminal cases falling 12.8% year-over-year in 2025 to their lowest level this century.

3. Explaining the need for a renewed drive, officials noted that organized crime remains stubborn and highly adaptable, with traditional gangs morphing into hidden, modernized operations. The campaign targets traditional "village bosses," syndicates involved in drugs and gambling, domestic-overseas collusion, and digital "soft violence" crimes like naked-chatting blackmail and public-opinion extortion. Authorities will aggressively seize illicit assets to destroy the economic foundations of these networks.

4. Legal scholars caution against repeating the mistakes of the previous campaign. Zhao Cong of Beijing Zhongwen Law Firm warned that some local authorities lowered evidentiary standards, inflated charges to meet quotas, and engaged in profit-driven enforcement that harmed legitimate private enterprises. He stated that prioritizing quotas over evidence-based adjudication will severely undermine the new crackdown's effectiveness.

5. Past wrongful convictions highlighted by Caixin illustrate these systemic issues. Real estate developer Zeng Jianbin was branded a gang leader and sentenced to 19 years before prosecutors withdrew the charges and the court overturned the ruling. A group of 13 construction workers led by Liang Ming was fully exonerated after four trials. Zhao noted that ordinary debt disputes were often escalated into organized crime cases based solely on petitions and fragmented confessions.

6. To prevent wrongful convictions, Zhao recommended an independent review mechanism demanding objective proof of stable hierarchies, consistent illicit revenue, and a pattern of criminal activity. He urged prosecutors to rigorously oversee police investigations and refuse to proceed to trial if reasonable doubt remains. He also emphasized prioritizing courtroom cross-examination and the exclusion of illegal evidence over reliance on confessions.

7. Addressing private property concerns, Zhao criticized the previous campaign's reliance on asset-seizure targets as a core performance metric. This led to the excessive freezing of corporate assets over minor regulatory breaches or improper debt collection, paralyzing compliant businesses. He argued that accurately distinguishing between actual criminal syndicates and businesses with irregular operations would protect the local business environment.

8. Top policymakers have taken note of these systemic issues. Chen Wenqing, head of the Central Political and Legal Affairs Commission, ordered strict adherence to the law, ensuring no innocent parties are wrongfully implicated to meet quotas. The Commission reiterated the presumption of innocence, high evidentiary standards, and the protection of defendants' and defense lawyers' legal rights throughout the campaign.
