From Abby Choi to the ‘Hello Kitty’ killing: Hong Kong’s most shocking crimes

SCMP | FREE | 1084 words | 2026-10-12

The alleged murder and dismemberment of Abby Choi Tin-fung in 2023, which prosecutors say was linked to a dispute over a luxury flat, has once again gripped Hong Kong as the trial over the 28-year-old socialite’s death entered its second week.

Choi’s ex-husband, Alex Kwong Kong-chi, 31, and his elder brother, Anthony Kwong Kong-kit, 35, have pleaded guilty to preventing the lawful burial of her body. Their father, Kwong Kau, 68, has pleaded not guilty to the same charge. All three men deny murdering Choi.

While Hong Kong has a reputation as one of the world’s safest cities, it has seen its share of sensational and gruesome crimes.

1. Plaza Hollywood stabbings (2023)

Just months after Choi’s alleged murder, two women were stabbed to death in a frenzied knife attack at the Plaza Hollywood shopping centre in Diamond Hill by a man suffering from severe mental illness.

News of the unprovoked attack, captured on CCTV, spread widely online and heightened safety concerns at a time when the city was still recovering from the Covid-19 pandemic.

Szeto Sing-kwong, 43, a former warehouse worker, wandered through the shopping centre with an unsheathed knife he had just bought before encountering Fong Hiu-tung, 26, and Lau Kai-hei, 22, outside a Sasa cosmetics shop.

CCTV footage showed Szeto repeatedly stabbing one of the women in an attack lasting nearly a minute. The second woman tried repeatedly to intervene but was herself slashed and stabbed.

Fong sustained 27 knife wounds and Lau eight, according to medical evidence. Both died from blood loss and respiratory failure.

Szeto later pleaded guilty to two counts of manslaughter by reason of diminished responsibility, after prosecutors accepted that an acute relapse of paranoid schizophrenia meant he should not be held liable for murder.

Last month, the High Court ordered him to receive indefinite mandatory psychiatric treatment and supervision at Siu Lam Psychiatric Centre in Tuen Mun, noting that he continued to show limited insight into his condition.

2. British banker turned double murderer (2014)

British banker Rurik Jutting tortured and killed two Indonesian women in his Wan Chai flat between October and November 2014.

Jutting tortured 23-year-old Sumarti Ningsih for three days, subjecting her to what the court described as increasingly cruel violence using a belt, sex toys, pliers and his fists, before slitting her throat on or about October 27. Her body was later found in a large black suitcase.

Five days later, he took 26-year-old Seneng Mujiasih, also known as Jesse Lorena Ruri, back to his flat and cut her throat.

It was reported that his murder spree was triggered by the break-up with his fiancée.

Jutting was sentenced to life imprisonment for two counts of murder in 2016. He later appealed to the Court of Final Appeal, which dismissed his final attempt after just eight minutes.

3. Son’s gruesome slaughter of parents (2013)

Henry Chau Hoi-leung, then 29, killed his parents in a rented flat in Tai Kok Tsui and placed their dismembered heads in a refrigerator.

Chau had planned to murder his father, Chau Wing-ki, 65, and mother, Siu Yuet-yee, 63, for six months. He told the court he hated his parents and blamed them for his failures in life and love.

He killed and dismembered the couple at a flat belonging to his friend, Angus Tse Chun-kei. Tse said Chau had threatened to harm his own mother, prompting him to help pack and dispose of some body parts at sea.

Chau was caught after sending text messages confessing to the killings, having earlier filed a missing persons report and made an online appeal for help finding his parents.

Forensic officers believed some of the couple’s body parts had been cooked, microwaved and salted. Others were decomposing when found.

In 2015, Chau was convicted of two counts of murder and sentenced to life imprisonment. Tse was sentenced to one year in prison for preventing the lawful burial of bodies.

The case inspired the 2022 film The Sparring Partner.

4. The ‘Hello Kitty’ case (1999)

In one of Hong Kong’s most horrifying cases, 23-year-old nightclub hostess Fan Man-yee was kidnapped and tortured by three triad members before her death. Her head was later placed inside a Hello Kitty doll.

Fan was abducted and held in a flat in Granville Road, Tsim Sha Tsui, after the men accused her of stealing HK$4,000 (US$510) from one of their wallets.

She was held for nearly a month in the flat, which was filled with Hello Kitty toys. The attackers, believed to have been under the influence of drugs, were suspected of torturing her in the days before her death.

After she died, the men dismembered her body, boiled the flesh to conceal the smell of decomposition and discarded other remains. They kept her skull and sewed it into the head of a large Hello Kitty toy.

The case came to light when a 13-year-old girl told police she had been having nightmares and felt “haunted” by a ghost. She was the girlfriend of one of the three killers and had occasionally joined them in assaulting Fan.

In exchange for immunity, she testified against the men, who were convicted of manslaughter. Because only Fan’s skull, a tooth and some internal organs were recovered, the court heard there was insufficient evidence to determine whether she had been murdered or died from another cause, such as a drug overdose.

The three men were sentenced in 2000 to life imprisonment for manslaughter, false imprisonment and preventing Fan’s lawful burial. One had his sentence reduced to 18 years after a retrial.

5. The ‘Rainy Night Butcher’ serial killings (1982)

Perhaps Hong Kong’s most infamous serial killer, night-shift taxi driver Lam Kor-wan murdered four women in the 1980s after picking them up as passengers on rainy evenings.

He drove them to secluded locations, strangled them with wires, photographed their bodies, dismembered them and preserved body parts in containers at his home.

Lam was arrested in 1982 after attempting to develop photographs of his victims at a Kodak shop in Mong Kok. The shop manager alerted police, who ambushed Lam when he returned to collect the pictures.

Although the photographs suggested there may have been more victims, Lam was ultimately charged with the murders of four women police were able to identify.

The court heard that he had eaten part of one victim’s intestine and, according to evidence, including photographs and videos, engaged in necrophilia.

Lam was sentenced to death, but the sentence was later commuted to life imprisonment.
