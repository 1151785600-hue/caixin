3 top reasons Trump will face a stronger Xi Jinping at November Apec forum in China

SCMP | FREE | 1215 words | 2026-09-27

When Donald Trump arrives in Shenzhen for the November Apec summit, he will face a Chinese leader even more confident than the one who left Washington this week after a state visit heavy on pageantry but light on concrete deliverables.

Experts attributed this confidence to three key factors – a newly upgraded bilateral framework seeking a “constructive relationship of strategic stability based on respect, fairness and reciprocity”, the warmer tone during Xi’s three-day visit, and – crucially – the significant leverage of host diplomacy.

In southern China’s Shenzhen – home to technological giants Huawei, BYD and Tencent – President Xi Jinping will have greater control over the agenda and showcase Beijing’s growing diplomatic clout in ways impossible during a bilateral summit.

Meanwhile, the Washington spectacle will reverberate with US allies and middle powers, who experts said would need to reassess their China strategies.

“As the Apec host, China will clearly possess far more autonomy in agenda-setting, topic selection, and preparatory work than it did during the Washington summit, where the US controlled the choreography while China held only participation and discussion rights,” said Chen Qi, director of the Centre for US-China Relations at Tsinghua University.

The shift in leverage would be particularly evident in the multilateral setting, he added.

“Beijing could also showcase its diplomatic influence and leadership far more effectively than in bilateral encounters.”

Statements released by both countries on Saturday confirmed that Trump intended to travel to Shenzhen in November to attend the Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation forum, setting up a third face-to-face meeting with Xi this year.

Ali Wyne, senior research and advocacy adviser for US-China relations at the International Crisis Group, said the Apec Economic Leaders’ Meeting would help Xi highlight “China’s growing economic and diplomatic sway – including with several US allies and partners” shortly after midterm elections likely to be bruising for Trump’s Republican Party.

“Xi appreciates how greatly Trump values opportunities to convey that the two leaders enjoy a strong rapport … Their Washington summit this week demonstrated his willingness to reverse or weaken US measures that might risk his meetings with Xi,” Wyne added.

Zhang Chi, senior researcher at the University of Hong Kong’s Centre on Contemporary China and the World (CCCW), called the upgraded relationship “a very successful strategic convergence” that placed Beijing and Washington on a fairer footing.

“Equally important is that China has demonstrated that attempts by Washington to reshape the bilateral power balance through ‘maximum pressure’ are ineffective and create uncontrollable problems,” he said.

Marking his second state visit to the US since 2015, Xi arrived to a rare tarmac greeting from President Donald Trump – a courtesy usually delegated to lower-level officials.

The 42 hours of summitry that followed saw both leaders hold up China as an equal or near-equal power to the United States. Trump spoke warmly of an emerging “great friendship”, while Xi portrayed the bilateral bond as a “river that never stops flowing” as its leaders work to “steer the giant ship” of relations forward.

Saturday’s statements by both countries further indicated a fourth Xi-Trump meeting this year, at the December Group of 20 summit in Miami. The pair last met in May, when Trump travelled to Beijing on a state visit.

Just ahead of the Washington summit, the two sides agreed to extend the bilateral trade truce set to expire in November by two months. As part of the truce, Washington has pledged to pause new tariffs while Beijing will suspend some rare earth export controls.

The unprecedented frequency of head-of-state interaction – four summits in eight months – signals a window of relative stability, according to Chinese experts.

The high-frequency meetings this year make policy continuity “both a promise and an inertia”, said Zhang Zhizhou, from the school of international relations and diplomacy at Beijing Foreign Studies University.

This would potentially help to shield Beijing from disruptive unilateral actions by Washington in the interim, he said, while identifying several factors contributing to China’s increased confidence heading into the Apec summit. These include China’s rising technological strength, growing influence in the Global South and Belt and Road Initiative partner nations.

All of this would make the Chinese side – which excels at host diplomacy – “more confident and bolster their negotiating position”, Zhang in Beijing said.

Shortly after Trump and Xi wrapped up their Washington summit, the White House announced that both sides had recommended reciprocal tariff relief covering US$30 billion worth of non-sensitive trade moving in each direction.

US exports slated for preferential treatment under the proposed framework include agricultural produce, seafood, timber, cosmetics and medical equipment.

The reciprocal Chinese list covers consumer goods such as small household appliances, toys, festive decorations and children’s car seats.

But experts also urged caution on long-term predictability, noting that the present strategy remained tied to Trump personally and domestic pressures and geopolitics could test the diplomatic momentum.

“Going forward, there are two key factors to watch: whether Trump can still honour his commitments once his hands are free, as upcoming midterm elections and the quagmire in Iran are currently draining his attention; and second, any provocative moves on Taiwan arms sales,” Zhang from CCCW said.

Trump, who has described arms sales to Taiwan as a negotiating tool with Beijing, postponed a planned US$14 billion package following his May summit with Xi.

The South China Morning Post reported this month that the US was considering postponing the announcement of arms sales to Taiwan until the end of the year, pointing to the potential Apec and G20 summits.

Beijing sees Taiwan as part of China to be reunited by force if necessary. Most countries, including the US, do not recognise self-ruled Taiwan as an independent state. But Washington is opposed to any attempt to take Taiwan by force and is committed to supplying it with weapons.

Ma Bo, associate professor at Nanjing University, said Washington was operating on two parallel tracks. While the White House projects cordiality at the top, the US security apparatus – aligning with its allies – continues to press ahead.

“Detente at the leader level does not bind Congress, technological export controls, or the actions of America’s regional allies,” Ma warned.

The current equilibrium is also seen as likely to prompt uneasy recalibration among middle powers and traditional US allies who watched Xi’s visit with a mix of relief and trepidation.

Xin Qiang, deputy director of the Centre for US Studies at Fudan University, noted that Washington’s regional partners – from Tokyo to Manila – were increasingly anxious about the potential erosion of US strategic support.

“As [US-China] ties stabilise, the backing these countries can extract from Washington will inevitably erode,” Xin said.

“If they attempt to use America to confront Beijing, they may find themselves without US endorsement, or even risk being left out in the cold.”

According to Zhang from CCCW, Europe is in an “embarrassing” position, as the progress in US-China ties will make Europe’s own escalation of trade pressure on Beijing look increasingly discordant.

Meanwhile, countries that recognise Beijing’s playbook in handling Washington – “those convinced that caving in to Trump brings no tangible benefit, will be the first to pivot towards Beijing with a more proactive and pragmatic posture”, he said.
