China set to decide if Costa Rican or Guyanese diplomat becomes first woman UN chief

SCMP | FREE | 1453 words | 2026-09-26

China is poised to decide which of two Latin American women becomes the first to lead the United Nations, after three rounds of informal voting in the Security Council failed to produce a favourite.

Several UN delegations that spoke to the South China Morning Post over the past three days said Beijing was weighing Rebeca Grynspan, the Costa Rican economist who heads UN Trade and Development, and Carolyn Rodrigues-Birkett, Guyana’s ambassador to the UN.

The same delegations said China had ruled out Rafael Grossi, the Argentine director general of the International Atomic Energy Agency. Grossi is widely seen as Washington’s preferred candidate and was nominated last year by Argentine President Javier Milei.

China has kept cordial but cooler relations with Milei since his 2023 presidential campaign, when he called the Chinese government “assassins” and promised to cut ties in favour of the “civilised side of life”.

Under UN rules, the 193-member General Assembly appoints the secretary general on the recommendation of the 15-member Security Council. The winner will succeed current UN chief Antonio Guterres, whose second term ends on December 31. The assembly has never rejected a name put forward by the council.

A candidate needs at least nine votes in the council and no veto from any of the five permanent members, which are China, France, Russia, the United Kingdom and the United States.

Before a formal vote, the council holds secret straw polls in which members mark each candidate “encourage”, “discourage” or “no opinion”. Colour-coded ballots, used in later rounds, show whether a discourage vote came from a permanent member, though not which one.

Both Grynspan and Rodrigues-Birkett travelled to Beijing this year to meet Foreign Minister Wang Yi, but China has not publicly endorsed either of them.

Grynspan led the first straw poll in July with 10 encourage votes, and Rodrigues-Birkett came first in the second round in August.

In the third poll a week ago, Grynspan received nine encourage votes, five discourage votes and one no opinion, according to diplomats with knowledge of the tally sheets. Rodrigues-Birkett received eight encourage votes, and her discourage votes rose from three to six.

Grossi, Ugandan diplomat and former UN under-secretary-general Olara Otunnu and Senegal’s former president Macky Sall received five encourage votes each. Former Ecuadorian foreign minister Maria Fernanda Espinosa received three, and Ecuador’s Ivonne A-Baki received none.

The results do not show whether any of Grynspan’s five discourage votes came from a permanent member. In the 2016 race, which took six straw polls, Guterres did not fall below 11 encourage votes.

China has not said publicly which candidate it prefers, but the Brics summit in New Delhi earlier this month could serve as a hint of what Beijing thinks of the contest.

During the summit, the Brics leaders adopted a joint declaration noting that “no woman has ever been elected for the role of Secretary-General” and that only one national from Latin America and the Caribbean had held the post.

Also, China’s ambassador to the UN, Fu Cong, reportedly said in May that Beijing supported regional rotation for the organisation’s top job and would be “pleased to see a woman in the post”, according to Costa Rican news site El Observador. Grynspan thanked him for the remarks at the time.

Bachelet’s withdrawal

While the choice between the leading female candidates appears wide open, another contender has categorically failed to win China’s favour.

While there is no confirmation on its position on Grynspan and Rodrigues-Birkett, three sources familiar with the matter told the SCMP that the Chinese delegation “decisively voted against” Michelle Bachelet, the former Chilean president.

Bachelet withdrew from the race last Saturday, a day after she received one encourage vote and 11 discourage votes in the third poll. Her withdrawal left seven officially nominated candidates in the race.

Chile’s government under President Jose Antonio Kast withdrew her nomination earlier in the year, and her candidacy continued with the backing of Brazilian President Luiz Inacio Lula da Silva and Mexico. Conservative US lawmakers had also urged Washington to veto her over her support for abortion rights, Reuters reported.

Bachelet’s relationship with Beijing deteriorated during her term as UN high commissioner for human rights. She visited Xinjiang in May 2022 and Western governments criticised the trip as too soft on China.

Then, minutes before her UN term ended in August that year, her office published an assessment of Xinjiang. It said abuses against Uygurs and other mostly Muslim minorities in the region “may amount to crimes against humanity”.

China’s mission in Geneva rejected the report as disinformation serving the US and other Western countries.

Meetings with Wang Yi

Grynspan met Wang in Beijing in June and, according to the Chinese foreign ministry, reaffirmed her commitment to the one-China principle. She also praised China’s support for multilateralism and the UN Charter.

Wang told her that her remarks showed an understanding of the concerns of Global South countries.

He said the next secretary general should uphold the UN Charter and be capable, fair and responsible, and that China would take part in the selection with a “constructive attitude”.

Grynspan has led UN Trade and Development since 2021. In 2022 she helped broker the Istanbul agreements, which reopened Ukrainian grain exports through the Black Sea and eased Russian food and fertiliser shipments.

Rodrigues-Birkett also met Wang in Beijing, although a month later. The ministry said she “expressed support for UN General Assembly Resolution 2758”, which Beijing cites as the basis of its claim to represent China at the UN, and “reaffirmed the one-China principle”.

She also praised Chinese President Xi Jinping’s four global initiatives and China’s proposal for a World Artificial Intelligence Cooperation Organisation. The Chinese-language readout quoted her as calling China “a leader on development issues”.

After the meeting, Guyana’s Department of Public Information reported that China supported her candidacy. It later apologised and said the report had “mischaracterised Beijing’s position”.

In February 2021, Guyana cancelled an agreement for a Taiwan office less than 24 hours after it was announced, following a public objection from the Chinese foreign ministry.

Yet, two sources from countries represented at the Security Council told the SCMP that Grynspan had a slight advantage with Beijing. But they said the Chinese delegation was wary of her because of her ties to Israel and her perceived closeness to Washington.

Chinese commentators have also pointed to Grynspan’s Jewish background and relatives in Israel. She would be the UN’s first Jewish secretary general.

Meanwhile, the New York Times reported this week that the US had indicated it would block Rodrigues-Birkett. The newspaper said the objection related to her statement to the Security Council in 2024 that “a veritable genocide” was taking place in Gaza.

A State Department official told the newspaper that the US wanted a candidate who would pursue substantial reform and streamline the organisation. The Guyanese government has not commented publicly on the report.

China’s mission to the UN and the US State Department did not immediately respond to requests for comment.

On Tuesday, Rodrigues-Birkett joined Guyanese President Irfaan Ali in New York as he signed the Shield of the Americas security communique with US President Donald Trump and Secretary of State Marco Rubio, on the sidelines of the General Assembly.

Next steps

The sources heard by SCMP said they expected the race to be discussed during Xi’s visit to Washington this week. They said it was not clear whether Xi would raise it with Trump or leave it to a meeting between Wang and Rubio.

Xi met Trump at the White House on Thursday but did not travel to New York for the General Assembly’s high-level week.

The race remains open, and diplomats said new compromise candidates who are acceptable to all five permanent members could still emerge if the deadlock persists. Some rumoured names could include Emirati diplomat and former UAE permanent representative to the UN Lana Nusseibeh , former Dutch minister Sigrid Kaag, and Kazakh President Kassym-Jomart Tokayev. None of them has said they would run.

The split among the permanent members has no precedent in recent UN selections, and it risks disrupting the organisation’s leadership if no successor is chosen by the end of the year.

Guterres has made clear he does not intend to stay on in an interim capacity while the council searches for a successor. He has already begun arranging his permanent return to Portugal, according to a UN source.

A fourth straw poll could be held after the high-level week ends on Monday. In 2006 and 2016, the council held its final straw polls in early October.
