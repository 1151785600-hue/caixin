Former Nanjing Museum Chief Jailed in Bribery Case Tied to Missing Masterpiece

Caixin Global | FULLTEXT | 305 words | 2026-09-17 03:10

1945：

Xu Huping was born.

1973：

Xu Huping joined the Nanjing Museum as a clerk after military service and a series of blue-collar jobs.

1990s：

Xu Huping bypassed appraisal and review procedures to approve the illegal transfer of Jiangnan Spring and other donated masterpieces to a provincial state-owned cultural relics store for sale.

1995：

A fifth work was sold for 16,000 yuan and remains unaccounted for as recovery efforts continue in 2026.

July 1997：

A clerk at the store colluded to buy the transferred scroll at an artificially low price before reselling it to a private dealer.

2001-2005：

Xu Huping served as director of the Nanjing Museum.

1959：

Jiangnan Spring, a celebrated scroll, was donated to the museum by the family of modern collector Pang Laichen.

Until 2008：

Xu Huping remained the museum's Communist Party secretary.

May 2025：

Jiangnan Spring appeared in a Beijing auction catalog with an estimated value of 88 million yuan.

2025：

Pang Shuling, Pang Laichen's great-granddaughter, alerted authorities, prompting the auction house to withdraw the work about an hour before bidding was set to begin.

2025：

After public outcry, the National Cultural Heritage Administration and the Jiangsu provincial government formed a joint task force in December 2025.

Late 2025：

A fourth painting, initially believed to be missing, was found in the museum's storage.

Dec. 23, 2025：

Authorities took Xu Huping from his home, the day the national investigation was announced.

2025 and 2026：

Several other provincial cultural officials faced dismissals, demotions, and severe warnings for dereliction of duty.

2026：

Xu Huping was sentenced to three years in prison and fined 200,000 yuan.

2026：

Xu Huping was expelled from the Communist Party and lost his retirement benefits.

2026：

Authorities recovered Jiangnan Spring and two other missing donated paintings through negotiations with their current holders.

Starting 2026：

Measures were implemented for tighter security around the Nanjing Museum's archives and a provincewide campaign to strengthen inventory controls at state-owned museums.
