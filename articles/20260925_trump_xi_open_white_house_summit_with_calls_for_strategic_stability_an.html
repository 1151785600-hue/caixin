Trump, Xi Open White House Summit With Calls for Strategic Stability and AI Guardrails

Caixin Global | FULLTEXT | 657 words | 2026-09-25 03:12

1. U.S. President Donald Trump welcomed Chinese leader Xi Jinping to the White House on Thursday for a three-day state visit, opening high-stakes talks aimed at stabilizing relations between the world's two largest economies amid long-running friction over trade, technology, and security.

]

. The South Lawn ceremony marked Xi's first White House visit in more than a decade, with both leaders framing the summit as a chance to establish durable guardrails; Trump emphasized shared commercial and technological interests, while Xi called for a "constructive strategic-stability relationship" to avoid zero-sum confrontation.

[para.

2

]

.

2. In his welcoming remarks, Trump expressed confidence that focusing on common interests could shape global peace and prosperity for decades.

[para.

3

]

. Xi strongly urged sustained engagement across all government levels, insisting that China and the U.S. must "hold the line of no conflict and no confrontation," explicitly rejecting the inevitability of the "Thucydides Trap" theory that an established power and rising rival are destined for war.

[para.

4

]

. He clarified that healthy competition is acceptable but must be kept within bounds—a race of catching up, not a wrestle where either wins or loses.

3. The ceremony itself featured full military honors, including an inspection of the Marine Corps Silent Drill Platoon, a review of 479 service members across all branches, and a flyover by a B-2 Spirit stealth bomber and four F-22 Raptor fighter jets. Trump and First Lady Melania Trump greeted Xi and his wife, Peng Liyuan, with both leaders wearing burgundy-toned ties.

[para.

]

4. Artificial intelligence emerged as a central focus of the agenda. Ahead of the meetings, Trump posted on social media that discussions would center on what he termed "super intelligence," arguing the technology should develop rapidly with the U.S. Department of Justice serving as a regulatory backstop.

Commercially, Trump noted that a bilateral trade council created during his May trip to Beijing had advanced a more balanced economic relationship, specifically citing efforts for broader market access for U.S. farmers and ranchers. Xi reiterated that China remains open to foreign investment, urging Washington to ensure fair treatment for Chinese firms and suggesting both sides "lengthen the list of cooperation and shorten the list of issues."

Xi outlined several concrete areas for progress: Beijing is prepared to deepen counternarcotics and law-enforcement cooperation, increase direct commercial flights, and maintain regular military dialogue to improve crisis communication. In a bid to reinforce people-to-people ties, Xi announced that Beijing will invite 100,000 young Americans to study in China over the next five years and will send two giant pandas, Pingping and Fushuang, to Zoo Atlanta.
