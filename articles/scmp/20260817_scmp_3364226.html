China’s Xi praises former president Jiang’s contribution in show of party unity

SCMP | FREE | 1143 words | 2026-08-17

President Xi Jinping led commemorations of the 100th anniversary of the birth of former state leader Jiang Zemin at a grand ceremony in Beijing, in a show of political continuity and party unity ahead of next year’s major power reshuffle.

In a 40-minute speech, Xi addressed thousands of officials and delegates from various sectors at the ceremony, held in the Great Hall of the People, delivering a speech that highlighted Jiang’s contribution to the party and the country.

A large portrait of Jiang was displayed above the rostrum – a practice reserved for deceased former top leaders like Mao Zedong and Deng Xiaoping – and all seven members of the Standing Committee of the Politburo, as well as a long list of retired state leaders, were present, in a show of solidarity within the party.

They included former premier Wen Jiabao, who served between 2003 to 2013, former vice-presidents Wang Qishan and Zeng Qinghong. Former president Hu Jintao was absent.

The ceremony was also attended by members of the Jiang family as well as people who worked around him, according to Premier Li Qiang, who moderated the event.

The commemorative event was hosted a year before the 21st Party Congress, scheduled for next autumn, when a new leadership line will be formed and Xi is set to renew another five-year term as the party’s leader.

Xi praised a long list of Jiang’s achievements, from political loyalty to the party to economic development, foreign policy and defence policy, with clear reference to his own governance style, including fighting corruption, a firm stance on the party’s unchallenged status to rule and decisive leadership when facing crisis.

“During the 13 years [of his leadership], the international situation was complex and volatile, and Jiang led the party leadership in unswervingly upholding the party’s line,” Xi said.

Xi called on the entire party to learn from Jiang’s “rock-solid political conviction”, praising him for his unwavering commitment to the Communist Party’s line and policies.

Xi has repeatedly articulated that the party and the country should have confidence in the path, system, theory and culture of socialism with Chinese characteristics.

Xi also mentioned that Jiang voluntarily stepped down from his party leadership positions in 2002.

The event was the latest to commemorate the 100th anniversary of Jiang’s birth, following a high profile 12-episode documentary that aired on state broadcaster CCTV from last week.

In Monday’s speech, Xi placed particular emphasis on Jiang’s courage and sense of responsibility in confronting crises – qualities he has also long urged party cadres to cultivate.

Xi highlighted the critical situation facing the party in the late 1980s and early 1990s – the period after the Tiananmen crackdown and collapse of the Soviet Union – saying Jiang “unswervingly upheld economic development as the central task” while sticking to “the Four Cardinal Principles”, which translate into upholding the party’s unchallenged rule.

“Jiang upheld reform and opening up, carried out rectification, engaged in diplomatic struggles, and resolutely safeguarded the country’s independence, dignity, security and stability,” Xi said.

Xi has long stressed the need to prevent the infiltration of Western ideology into China and has highlighted what he sees as the threat it poses to national security and stability.

Xi also praised Jiang for never making concessions on issues of sovereignty, listing achievements under Jiang including the handover of Hong Kong in 1997 and Macau in 1999. Next year will mark the 30th anniversary of Hong Kong’s return to Chinese rule.

Xi also praised Jiang for his tough stance against “Taiwan independence”.

“Jiang promoted the reaching of the 1992 consensus between the two sides of the Taiwan Strait, embodying the one-China principle, and vigorously carried out the struggle against separatism and Taiwan independence,” he said.

Under the oral understanding, reached in 1992 during Jiang’s tenure between Beijing and Taiwan’s then-ruling Kuomintang, both sides agreed there was “one China”, but differed over how that “one China” should be interpreted.

He also praised the former leader for recognising the “strategic opportunity period” faced by China, announced in 2002 following the country’s acession to the World Trade Organization.

Beijing sees Taiwan as part of China to be reunited by force if necessary. Most countries, including the US, do not see Taiwan as a state but are opposed to change of status quo by force.

In the speech, Xi praised Jiang for managing major risks facing the country, including political and economic risks as well as natural disasters.

He singled out Jiang’s management of the 1997 Asian financial crisis, as well as the massive flooding event of 1998.

Xi also praised Jiang for “calmly responding to a series of international emergencies concerning China’s sovereignty and security” – an apparent reference to the 1999 Nato bombing of the Chinese embassy in Yugoslavia as well as the 2001 collision between a Chinese fighter jet and a US surveillance aircraft over the South China Sea.

Xi further highlighted Jiang’s continued support for successive leaders after his retirement in 2004, particularly in the “fight against corruption”. Anti-corruption has been one of Xi’s central priorities since he came to power in 2012.

The event concluded with a standing salute to the Internationale.

Xie Maosong, a senior researcher at the National Institute of Strategic Studies, Tsinghua University, said Xi’s address served as “a retrospective on Jiang’s life while highlighting the points of continuity and alignment with the party’s priorities today”.

Xi’s praise of Jiang’s mandate – specifically that “governing the country requires governing the party first, and governing the party must be strict” – echoes the theme of the coming 20th Central Committee’s fifth plenum in October, Xie noted.

The Politburo announced in July that the conclave would focus on “consistently exercising full and rigorous governance over the party”.

Under the same slogan, the anti-corruption campaign has disgraced senior officials of higher ranks and at a faster speed than many thought possible, with few signs of slowing down as it enters its 15th year under Xi.

According to Xie, Xi’s speech also signalled continuity on economic reform from Jiang’s era.

“Adapting to the requirements of time-honoured development and practical needs, [Jiang] continuously advanced theoretical innovation, institutional innovation, technological innovation, cultural innovation and innovations in all other areas,” Xi said in the speech.

The reference reaffirmed Beijing’s current focus on high-quality development and innovation-driven growth, Xie noted.

By recalling how Jiang navigated severe domestic and external turbulence without wavering on party authority or core sovereignty, the intention was to demand that today’s leadership possess a tenacious “fighting spirit” and superior fighting skills, Xie said.

These would be needed to navigate the tests of “high winds, rushing waves, and even perilous, stormy seas” when “confronting unprecedented global changes in a century and complex geopolitical dynamics such as US-China relations”, he added.
