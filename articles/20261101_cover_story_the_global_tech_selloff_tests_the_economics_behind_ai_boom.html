Cover Story: The Global Tech Sell-Off Tests the Economics Behind AI Boom

Caixin Global | FULLTEXT | 725 words | 2026-11-05 14:02

1. Anthropic's public stock filing, published Oct. 26, gave global technology shares a temporary lift—Philadelphia Semiconductor Index +2.82%, Japan's Nikkei 225 +1.73%, South Korea's Kospi +3.09%—yet its unexpected second-quarter operating turnaround merely interrupted two severe selloffs in July and October that erased hundreds of billions from the AI supply chain.

Losses have been steep across the sector: Nvidia surrendered more than $200 billion from its $5.43 trillion peak; SK Hynix tumbled over 50%; CoreWeave shed over $35 billion; Hong Kong-listed Zhipu AI and MiniMax fell more than 80% from peaks, with MiniMax sinking below its offer price. Anthropic's planned offering—potentially tens of billions near a $100 billion valuation—illustrates the precarious balance between bolstering AI commercialization confidence and absorbing scarce liquidity from strained equity markets.

3. According to JPMorgan strategist Nikolaos Panigirtzoglou, October's downturn showed no floor: dip-buyers were conspicuously absent, corporate buybacks were scaled back, and historically lean short positions precluded short-covering rallies. GF Securities' Kuang Shi attributed the decline to capital flows and sentiment rather than deteriorating fundamentals, noting investors now scrutinize capex sustainability, ARR at Anthropic and OpenAI, and commercially viable enterprise applications beyond automated coding.

4. Nvidia sits at the nexus of "circular financing" concerns—it has committed over $50 billion across model developers, neocloud operators like CoreWeave and Nscale, and optical suppliers. On Oct. 27, Nvidia announced a $3 billion investment in new Meta equity, with Meta expanding compute procurement. LSEG characterized this as an interconnected network obscuring end-user demand; Nomura likened the capex race to a prisoner's dilemma. In the two weeks after Oct. 6, the Philadelphia Semiconductor Index fell roughly 13%, China's STAR 50 dropped 24% from its June high, Nvidia retreated 11%, SK Hynix fell 20%, and Arm lost 24%.

5. Capital strains are most acute among "neoclouds." CoreWeave's revenue surged sevenfold to $1.9 billion in 2024 (62% from Microsoft), but regulatory filings revealed about $51.6 billion in total debt and lease obligations against only $5 billion in equity. Second-quarter operating cash flow of $679 million fell far short of $9.4 billion in adjusted capex; Bernstein estimated $76 billion in additional outside capital is needed. Yields on CoreWeave's unsecured notes climbed toward 15%, credit-default swaps blew out to roughly 1,100 basis points, and equity surrendered over 40% in two weeks. Physical bottlenecks compound financial risks—NEXTDC cut A$140 million from fiscal 2027 guidance after grid delays (stock plunged 41.7%), Thailand suspended 49 data centers, and Microsoft terminated its Nscale agreement in the UK.

6. Hyperscalers posted strong top line but massively increased capex: Alphabet spent $51.4 billion (free cash flow negative), Amazon spent $77.3 billion (up 120%), Microsoft's annual capex reached nearly $116 billion (up 80%). Meta's CEO—despite its Muse assistant reaching No. 1 on Apple's App Store—posted negative third-quarter free cash flow of $2.12 billion and unveiled a $35 billion equity offering on Oct. 26. Chinese developers Zhipu AI (revenue up ~400% to 954 million yuan) and MiniMax (up 283%) narrowed losses but remained deeply cash-flow negative. Their valuation multiples contracted from over 250 times ARR at peaks to roughly 21 times (Zhipu) and eight times (MiniMax), versus Anthropic's implied 22-29 times. Zhipu's gross margin fell to 26.4% from 50% a year earlier.

7. China's IPO market has chilled: Hong Kong hosted 116 debuts in the first nine months (up 76% YoY, HK$387.9 billion raised), but by September four out of five mid-month debutants broke offer prices. More than 380 companies await listing hearings; foreign funds logged HK$15.9 billion net outflows in early October. Moonshot AI confidentially submitted a Hong Kong application; DeepSeek is preparing for Shanghai's STAR Market. Robotics startups face skepticism—Leju Robotics suspended its Shenzhen IPO review, Fine Motion withdrew its Shanghai application, and several Hong Kong listings were put on hold. Robotics funding fell from 91 deals totaling 31.32 billion yuan in August to 19 deals totaling 4.93 billion yuan in early October.

8. Meanwhile, foundation models are upending the robotics value chain. Unitree Robotics' founder demonstrated GPT-6 Astra controlling a bipedal robot without task-specific tuning, projecting general-purpose household robots by 2027. Yet generalized foundation models threaten startups whose core product is a proprietary robotic "brain"—a robotics technician warned that companies focused only on software face a perilous position, as general-purpose AI risks rendering specialized models obsolete.
