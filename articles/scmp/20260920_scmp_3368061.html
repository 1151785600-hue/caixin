How will Beijing receive the most senior Taiwan official at Apec events this year?

SCMP | FREE | 1023 words | 2026-09-20

In a rare development, Taiwanese Labour Minister Hung Sun-han is expected to travel to Nanjing on Sunday, becoming the first cabinet-level official under William Lai Ching-te’s administration to visit mainland China.

Hung will attend an Asia-Pacific Economic Cooperation (Apec) ministerial meeting on Monday to discuss employment, vocational training, social security and the impact of artificial intelligence on jobs.

Hung will be the most senior Taiwanese official to visit the mainland for Apec events this year. The island’s vice-minister of economic affairs, Lai Chien-hsin, took part in the energy ministerial meeting in Beijing last week and then chief trade negotiator Yang Jen-ni participated in a trade ministers’ meeting in Suzhou in May.

While Hung’s visit is being closely watched for signs of a possible thaw in a prolonged cross-strait stand-off, analysts have cautioned against interpreting it as an icebreaking trip or a resumption of official exchanges between Taipei and Beijing.

The trip will also take place just days before US President Donald Trump is expected to host Chinese leader Xi Jinping in Washington, putting Taiwan under renewed scrutiny as the two powers seek to manage their differences.

Ahead of his departure, Hung stressed that Taiwan was a full Apec member under the name “Chinese Taipei”. He said he would take part under the grouping’s established arrangements and the principles of “equal participation and reciprocal dignity”.

Nevertheless, with official cross-strait dialogue suspended, Hung’s visit carries symbolic weight because no cabinet minister – particularly a political appointee – has travelled to the mainland since Lai took office in May 2024.

“A small step by Hung Sun-han would be a big step for cross-strait relations,” said Lee Yen-hsiu, a lawmaker from the main opposition Kuomintang, a Beijing-friendly party.

Lee questioned whether the trip signalled a change in the ruling Democratic Progressive Party’s (DPP) approach towards Beijing after more than two years of promoting its “resist China, oppose China” agenda.

She said Hung’s visit could be seen as “a possible test by Lai of whether cross-strait relations could begin to thaw”.

DPP lawmaker Wu Szu-yao urged Hung to remain alert to “potential personal safety concerns and the possibility of unequal treatment or politically motivated difficulties” while on the mainland.

But DPP legislative caucus whip Chuang Jui-hsiung dismissed such concerns.

“Are you kidding? If holding Apec in China meant there was a threat to someone’s personal safety, it wouldn’t just shock Taiwan – the whole world would be shocked by China,” Chuang said.

Analysts said Taipei might hope the trip created an impression of easing tensions, particularly ahead of the Washington summit.

“It is especially important before the sensitive Trump-Xi meeting to create an impression that Taiwan is not a troublemaker and hopes cross-strait relations can ease,” said Wang Kung-yi, head of the Taiwan International Strategic Study Society, a Taipei-based think tank.

But Wang said the trip could not be regarded as a genuine advance because Hung was attending a multilateral meeting and not direct talks with Beijing.

“If there were really a breakthrough, there would inevitably be substantive interactions, such as Hung meeting mainland officials,” he said.

Li Da-jung, a professor of international relations at Tamkang University in New Taipei City, similarly argued that the arrangement was standard.

“Taiwan is a full member of Apec, and to maintain its position in the international organisation, it has to send officials to attend the meeting,” Li said, adding that participation also helped maintain the island’s international visibility.

More important for Taipei, he said, would be the consequences of the coming Trump-Xi summit.

“Its impact on Taiwan remained uncertain, but Taipei would be watching closely to see whether Washington’s approach to the island – including pending arms sales – is affected by Trump’s talks with Xi,” Li said.

Analysts also doubted Beijing would create difficulties for Hung over his entry arrangements.

“Long-established arrangements have already governed participation in Apec meetings, and this is mainland China’s third time hosting the leaders’ meeting after Shanghai in 2001 and Beijing in 2014,” Wang said.

Hung should therefore be able to enter using his Apec invitation letter under existing arrangements instead of using a mainland-issued Taiwan compatriot permit, he added.

Beijing has skirted questions over whether Hung must hold the permit.

“Since 1991, Taiwan’s participation in Apec meetings and activities has been handled in accordance with the ‘one-China principle’ as well as the provisions and established practices of relevant Apec memorandums of understanding,” Chen Binhua, a spokesman for the State Council’s Taiwan Affairs Office, said last week.

Zhu Fenglian, a spokeswoman for the Taiwan Affairs Office, reiterated on Wednesday that under the one-China principle, the “Taiwan region” took part in Apec under the name “Chinese Taipei” and as a regional economy.

Taipei rejects that interpretation, saying its 1991 Apec accession memorandum contains no reference to the “one-China principle” and provides for participation on an equal basis with other members.

Beijing sees Taiwan as part of China to be reunited by force if necessary. Most countries, including the US, do not recognise Taiwan as an independent state, but Washington is opposed to any attempt to take the self-ruled island by force and is committed to supplying it with weapons.

Wang said the DPP could benefit politically if Beijing created difficulties for Hung – and thus Beijing was unlikely to take action that could inadvertently help the party electorally.

Huang Kwei-bo, a diplomacy professor at National Chengchi University in Taipei, said the DPP government was likely to stress that Hung was attending an international meeting rather than engaging in bilateral cross-strait exchanges.

He also cautioned against using the trip as a barometer of cross-strait relations because Beijing, as this year’s Apec host, was expected to invite representatives from all member economies, and Hung was only visiting because the Apec meeting happened to be held on the mainland.

Huang said Taipei’s choice of representative to the Apec Economic Leaders’ Meeting in Shenzhen in November would be a more significant test.
