At the sixth plenary session of the 20th Central Commission for Discipline Inspection (CCDI) in Beijing on Aug. 22, Xinhua reported, China’s top anti-graft body promoted two seasoned officials: Zhao Shiyong, 60, was elected a deputy secretary, while Zhang Zhong, 58, was elected both a standing committee member and a deputy secretary, raising both to ministerial level.

The reshuffle comes as the central leadership stresses strict party governance and systemic crackdowns on corruption in finance, healthcare and local government. It also highlights the institutional evolution of the CCDI, the Communist Party’s highest internal oversight body, whose sweeping anti-corruption campaign has shaped the political landscape for over a decade.

Zhao’s career reflects Beijing’s centralized approach to cadre discipline. Born in Sichuan, he earned a doctorate in agronomy and spent 18 years in the provincial agriculture department, becoming deputy director in 2007. He moved into local government in 2008, holding executive posts in Liangshan, Suining and Deyang.

His local tenure was marked by two crisis-management assignments after officials fell from power: in 2014 he was nominated mayor of Suining just six days after predecessor He Huazhang’s downfall, and in May 2018 he became party chief of Deyang, replacing Pu Bo, who faced severe disciplinary action just over 100 days after promotion.

Zhao was transferred to the eastern economic powerhouse of Jiangsu in January 2020 as vice governor, later serving briefly as provincial party secretary-general in 2021 before taking over the provincial Organization Department. In October 2022 he moved to Beijing as a CCDI standing committee member, and by December he headed the Organization Department of the CCDI and the National Commission of Supervision. That department, restructured in 2014, has significant power to nominate and assess provincial-level discipline chiefs in coordination with the central Organization Department, centralizing control over the anti-graft personnel system.

Zhang Zhong’s path differs, rooted in state-owned enterprise management and central personnel administration. Born in Shandong, he is now the youngest CCDI deputy secretary and the first person born after 1965 to hold the position. After studying water supply and drainage engineering, he spent 14 years with municipal state-owned construction and real estate companies, then became a deputy district mayor in Beijing in 2006. From 2008, he spent 11 years at the powerful Central Organization Department, including roles as director of its internal affairs management bureau and head of the cadre building and planning office. In March 2019 he shifted to discipline inspection as a deputy ministerial-level inspector on central inspection teams.

Later that year, Zhang became secretary of the Jilin Provincial Discipline Inspection Commission, where he worked to clear the lingering political influence of fallen senior officials. In July 2023 he moved south to Jiangsu to lead its Discipline Inspection Commission; there, he reported targeting officials with distorted views of political performance and power. Under his watch, the provincial body acted against a former Changzhou party chief and a former director of the provincial Development and Reform Commission, focusing on finance, state-owned enterprises, education, healthcare, land development and engineering bidding, resulting in 51 individuals being placed under formal retention measures.
