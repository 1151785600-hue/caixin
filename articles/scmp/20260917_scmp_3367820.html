Teodoro for president? Philippine defence chief draws 2028 election buzz after China clash

SCMP | FREE | 1050 words | 2026-09-17

Philippine Defence Secretary Gilberto Teodoro Jnr has won a burst of online support among Filipinos who see him as one of Manila’s most outspoken senior officials against China, with some already asking whether it could give him enough political momentum to carry him into the 2028 elections.

His latest boost came after a viral moment at the Seoul Defence Dialogue on September 8, where he read out a handwritten note reiterating Beijing’s rejection of the 2016 South China Sea arbitration ruling while on stage.

Forum organisers said the note came from a military attaché at the Chinese embassy in Seoul, who had passed it to a staff member and asked that it be given to Teodoro, according to South Korean news agency Yonhap.

Teodoro read parts of the note aloud, including Beijing’s position that the 2016 award was “illegal, null and void”, before saying the move was disrespectful to the forum’s South Korean hosts and showed disregard for international law.

“So, perhaps basic decency and behaviour is what China needs to do … This is actual coercion, bullying and aggression,” he said.

Beijing hit back, with Chinese foreign ministry spokeswoman Mao Ning accusing Philippine officials of “grandstanding and lying” and urging “certain people in the Philippines” to stop undermining China-Philippines relations and stability in the South China Sea.

China does not accept the 2016 ruling by an arbitral tribunal in The Hague, which found Beijing’s claims to historic rights within the nine-dash line had no legal basis under the United Nations Convention on the Law of the Sea (Unclos).

Beijing argues the tribunal had no jurisdiction because the case involved territorial sovereignty and maritime delimitation and says the Philippines abused the Unclos dispute settlement mechanism by bringing the case unilaterally.

The Seoul episode followed Beijing’s June sanctions on Teodoro, his wife and child over what China said were erroneous remarks that undermined its interests and damaged bilateral relations.

The move barred them from mainland China, Hong Kong and Macau and restricted Chinese organisations and individuals from dealing with them, but Teodoro remained defiant, saying the sanctions would not stop him from doing his job.

Even before his latest viral moment, some social media users in the Philippines had begun urging Teodoro to run for president, vice-president or the Senate in 2028.

On Facebook, a page entitled “Filipinos for Gibo Teodoro 2028” had sprung up by June and had 35,000 followers as of the time of writing. One user commented “if Sec Gibo decides to run for President, we should all support him”, while another said: “He’s my President in 2028.”

His Seoul response also drew support from public figures, including Senator Erwin Tulfo, who said Teodoro had done “the right thing”, and Senator Panfilo Lacson, who said the episode showed what Teodoro’s “brilliant mind” could bring to the world stage.

But political observers said Teodoro’s path to higher office would depend on whether he could translate a burst of online attention into sustained support – a challenge made clearer by his previous unsuccessful runs for national office.

Teodoro was criticised over disaster rescue operations after Typhoon Ketsana in 2009, when he was defence secretary, before running the following year as president Gloria Macapagal Arroyo’s official candidate and finishing in fourth place.

After that defeat, he largely shunned politics until 2022, when he offered to be the vice-presidential running mate of Davao City mayor Sara Duterte-Carpio.

When Duterte-Carpio decided to seek the vice-presidency herself, Teodoro instead ran for one of 12 Senate seats up for election that year under the poorly funded People’s Reform Party, but finished 15th and missed out.

Dr Ranjit Rye, founding president of poll firm Octa Research, told This Week in Asia that Teodoro was “not yet” ripe for the vice-presidential race but said a Senate run could be more realistic.

In Octa’s July poll of the public’s senatorial preferences, Teodoro placed No 29, with 11.2 per cent of respondents saying they would vote for him, while WR Numero Research’s June survey put him at No 35, with 6 per cent support.

Barry Gutierrez, who was spokesman to former vice-president Leni Robredo and is himself an ex-congressman, said survey numbers were “the most immediate measure” of viability, though not the only one.

“Unfortunately, Secretary Teodoro’s numbers are fairly low across the board at this point,” Gutierrez told This Week in Asia, adding that Teodoro “is not even considered in publicly released presidential surveys”.

“To me this indicates that, as of now, he is not top of mind when it comes to choices for the top posts in 2028,” he said.

Ronnie Holmes, Pulse Asia president and a professor of political science and development studies at De La Salle University, said the pollster “will include his name for a long list on presidential preference in our third-quarter survey”.

“I can’t comment on viability until we see the survey results,” Holmes said.

Gutierrez said Teodoro’s numbers could improve if his recent prominence became “the start of a more sustained visibility as a national leader”, but added that this would require “a sustained and consistent effort over time, and not simply one viral moment two years before the elections”.

“Gilberto Teodoro is qualified to run for higher office in 2028. He has the credentials and experience,” said Manuel Mogato, a defence and security analyst.

“But experience, expertise, and his high qualifications are not enough. Winnability is also important,” Mogato said, adding that current opinion polls suggested Teodoro still had significant ground to make up before 2028.

University of the Philippines political-science professor Dr Jean Franco said Teodoro was “definitely viable given his background, experience, and competence”, but that his association with Macapagal Arroyo, elite family background and lack of mass appeal could count against him.

“I believe, however, that if he is paired with a strong presidential candidate and if he has good campaign strategists, he can be a strong contender. He can carry the China issue, which I believe is something that resonates across classes,” she said.

Asked by radio station DZBB on September 11 whether he was eyeing an elective post, Teodoro said he was keeping his options open because “you know it’s hard to plan now. It’s hard to plan because I am focused on my work here”.
