Slain model Abby Choi called ‘birthing machine’ by ex-father-in-law, court hears

SCMP | FREE | 1022 words | 2026-10-18

The former father-in-law of slain Hong Kong socialite Abby Choi Tin-fung called her a “birthing machine” and tried to assault her before he allegedly plotted with his two sons to kill her three years ago, a court has heard.

Choi’s partner, Chris Tam Fong-chun, recounted on Tuesday a bitter argument between the influencer and her former in-laws in what he said was a watershed moment leading to a breakdown in the relationship in mid-2022.

Choi was previously married to Alex Kwong Kong-chi, 31, who is standing trial on a joint count of murder alongside his brother Anthony Kwong Kong-kit, 35, and his father Kwong Kau, 68.

Testifying for the first time in the High Court, Tam also said Choi had given his family a false impression that her former in-laws were leading a good life, because she feared her two children from her previous marriage would be disparaged in their new home due to their background.

Tam said he was not surprised initially to find out that Kwong Kau was the registered owner of a HK$72 million (US$9.17 million) flat at the Kadooria residential complex in Ho Man Tin, before she told him the flat was in fact bought using her money.

“When I saw the Kadooria flat was in Kwong Kau’s name, I did not deliberately ask [Choi] about it because she had been telling me all along that this family was doing OK,” Tam said.

Kwong allegedly resented Choi for trying to sell the upscale property and enlisted his two sons to help kill the 28-year-old on February 21, 2023. Prosecutors argued the flat was only registered in his name to avoid paying HK$9 million in double stamp duty.

The elder Kwong, a retired police sergeant, has also denied preventing the burial of Choi’s body, an offence to which his two sons pleaded guilty before the trial began last month.

Tam said his family’s wealth grew significantly after his father, who founded the popular restaurant chain TamJai Yunnan Mixian, received part of the profit from selling the brand to a Japanese company for HK$1 billion.

He said he first met Choi around the end of 2013 and early 2014, when he briefly returned from Australia during a break in his studies to meet up with her then-husband Alex Kwong, his former classmate.

He said Alex Kwong “totally disappeared” after one day in 2015, the same year he began the process to divorce Choi and absconded from criminal proceedings arising from his alleged involvement in a HK$6.3 million theft case.

Tam said he did not probe further after Choi said her then-partner was safe, because he thought she had somehow managed to find a way to contact him.

“I came out very often with Abby afterwards. We took care of her children together, and love grew between us as time went by,” he added.

The court heard Tam and Choi formally started dating in December 2015 and held a wedding banquet a year later, although they never registered their marriage. The model gave birth to their daughter in September 2016, and their son was born three years later.

Choi lived with her partner’s family at a luxurious residence on Begonia Road in Kowloon Tong and received up to HK$300,000 monthly from Tam’s father to cover the couple’s expenses.

Tam said the two children Choi had with Alex Kwong lived separately with two domestic helpers initially at a flat in the Laguna Verde estate in Hung Hom, before the socialite decided to move them to the Kadooria development in 2019 because it was closer to her Kowloon Tong home.

The witness highlighted a heated argument between Choi and her former mother-in-law, Jenny Li Sui-heung, on a day when the influencer’s elder daughter had a fever at the Kadooria home in June or July 2022.

According to Tam, the model was angry at Li for telling Alex Kwong to stay away from the child because she could infect him.

“My understanding is that Abby was at that time accusing Li Sui-heung of preferring sons to daughters,” he said.

Kwong Kau, who joined the row, reportedly lunged at Choi and hurled curses at her after he accused the influencer of assaulting his wife.

“And then Kwong Kau said: ‘Who do you think you are? You’re just a birthing machine’,” Tam continued.

Tam said the elder Kwong attempted to attack Choi a second time, but he intervened to prevent the former police officer from hitting the model.

He said Choi then barred Kwong Kau from visiting the residence. She also told Tam they needed to cut ties with the Kwong family, whom she described as horrible and ungrateful.

Defence lawyers also grilled a police officer helping to lead the investigation of the case.

Detective Constable Ko Yan-ki, who was originally not expected to take the witness box, was cross-examined over his text conversations with Choi’s mother, Cheung Yin-fa, in the months preceding the trial.

The messaging logs were produced as evidence after the officer contacted Cheung while she was giving evidence last week about her purported ownership of a flat at Whampoa Garden. She later clarified that she did not own the property.

Ko rejected the prosecution’s suggestion that he should have avoided contact with Cheung while her testimony was ongoing, saying he needed to make prompt inquiries on new information regarding her property ownership.

The messaging records also showed that Cheung had a monetary dispute with a friend she met at a mahjong parlour earlier this year.

Cheung said she was alleged to have owed HK$1.6 million, but she insisted the debt was settled seven years ago. She also revealed that she once lost “a few tens of millions of dollars” while gambling at a mahjong parlour in Jordan.

Ko said Cheung’s alleged debts did not catch his attention when he investigated the case, adding that he could not ascertain whether her claims about gambling millions of dollars away were true or exaggerated.

The trial continues before Deputy Judge Brian Keith and a nine-member jury.
