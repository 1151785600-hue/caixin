Chinese CEOs fly to US on own, await invites to Trump’s state dinner for Xi Jinping

SCMP | FREE | 1001 words | 2026-09-25

A small group of Chinese CEOs holding US visas has arrived in Washington after travelling separately from President Xi Jinping’s official entourage, according to multiple sources familiar with the matter.

But with the clock ticking and the business optics of the Chinese leader’s White House visit hanging on the final twists of diplomatic negotiations, the executives are still waiting to find out whether they will secure a seat at Thursday’s state dinner for Xi.

“Beijing’s security protocol is very different,” one of the sources said, noting that the executives did not fly with Xi’s official party or aboard his aircraft, unlike the American CEOs who arrived in Beijing alongside President Donald Trump in May.

Two sources said it was unlikely any Chinese business leader applied for a new visa for the summit. They also said the final decision on whether the Chinese corporate chiefs would join state visit events was tied in part to talks between US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent and Chinese Vice-Premier He Lifeng, and the matter came up during their meeting on Wednesday.

Bessent and He met that day at the World Bank in Washington, their second encounter in four days after talks on Sunday in New York to tackle what Bessent called “unfinished business” ahead of the leadership summit.

After Wednesday’s meeting, Bessent announced that the two sides had agreed to extend the “Busan arrangement” on trade – reached after Xi-Trump talks in the South Korean city – by two months, until January 10.

While US officials had played down the prospect of an extension being announced this week, the South China Morning Post had reported in August that an extension was “almost certain”.

Two sources said the extension had left the door open for the executives to appear at the state dinner and at possible side events.

However, their presence might ultimately yield more optics than substance, according to one source. They said the small delegation was unlikely to deliver the soft-power punch Beijing had hoped for, and some executives could even use the occasion to voice their dissatisfaction with US investment restrictions directly to officials. Indeed, the CEOs’ attendance was largely a formality, the source said.

Another source familiar with the matter said Trump’s priority remained negotiating the new Board of Investment, rendering efforts to seat Chinese CEOs at the state dinner a “lower policy priority”.

For Beijing, however, the optics matter.

Having Chinese CEOs at the state dinner would help to balance the presence of US technology bosses and put China’s own corporate and technological muscle on display. The Chinese side viewed business leaders taking part in the banquet and other events as a message that the world’s two largest economies were intertwined, one of the sources noted.

“I think given the current circumstances, some Chinese business representation at the state banquet is possible. Even if that didn’t take place, the American business leaders will arrange some gathering with them,” the source said.

The same source said some of the names under discussion included executives from Alibaba, Xiaomi, CATL and BYD. Alibaba owns the SCMP.

Another source said the backup group was likely to draw from Chinese electric-vehicle companies, which were the keenest to explore investment opportunities in the United States.

According to one source, some American business leaders with substantial commercial interests in China were likely to entertain their Chinese counterparts privately if they did not make it to the banquet. Several top executives from Chinese artificial intelligence companies were foregoing the summit over security concerns, the source said.

Another insider said the White House banquet venue could seat up to 100 guests, while the Chinese side had been allotted about 30 seats. In the event that the Chinese business leaders could not attend, the administration would invite a few more US guests, tapping into strong demand for the limited seats.

On the US side, Tesla CEO Elon Musk, Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang and Apple’s Tim Cook are among the business leaders confirmed to be attending Thursday’s dinner, after accompanying Trump to Beijing four months ago. OpenAI chief executive Sam Altman – who was not part of Trump’s delegation in May – is also on the state dinner guest list.

The May trip has become a heavily debated template for US-China diplomacy. Trump brought about 18 major chief executives with him, with Huang famously joining Air Force One at the last minute during a refuelling stop in Alaska.

While their visit yielded no headline corporate deals, an informal gathering took place between Chinese and American counterparts in Beijing, and some US executives stayed on for a few days after Trump’s departure. Trump also had several CEOs come to the bilaterals simply to be introduced.

Something similar was discussed for Xi’s reciprocal visit to Washington but was no longer on the table, sources said.

The SCMP reported on Tuesday that differences over possible investment announcements had created roadblocks for a Chinese business delegation potentially accompanying Xi.

A source familiar with the discussions said both sides had yet to reach common ground over the Board of Investment, particularly regarding potential Chinese ventures.

Another source said a lack of communication and differing interpretations of the reciprocity principle had derailed the original plan.

Beijing viewed the presence of Musk, Huang and Cook in Trump’s May delegation as a standard that should be mirrored in Washington. The US side, the source said, treated reciprocity as applying only to senior leadership. Poor logistics and a lack of formal engagement or arranged meetings for Chinese businesses coming to Washington added to the uncertainty.

The last-minute uncertainty over the state dinner follows the same pattern the SCMP described in August. Sources said then that Chinese officials were rattled by disorganisation and poor coordination inside the Trump White House in the weeks before Xi’s trip, and that they had already watched the same chaos play out ahead of the US leader’s May state visit to China.
