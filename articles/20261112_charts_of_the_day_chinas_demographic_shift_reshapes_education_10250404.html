Charts of the Day: China’s Demographic Shift Reshapes Education

Caixin Global | FULLTEXT | 498 words | 2026-11-12 10:41

2020 to 2025：

Preschool enrollments dropped by roughly one-third; senior high school students surged by 21.9%, with a 22.7% increase in teachers; primary school enrollments declined, but primary teachers remained slightly higher than in 2020. The gross enrollment rate for preschools rose from 85.2% to 92.9%.

Between 2020 and 2025：

Urban primary school students increased by 20.8%, with teacher counts rising by 25.4%; urban junior high school students increased by 33.3%, while teachers grew by 28.3%. The student-to-teacher ratio in urban primary schools dropped from 18.28 to 17.61; urban junior high schools saw their ratio rise from 13.04 to 13.55. In 23 of 31 provincial-level regions, urban junior high school student-to-teacher ratios climbed.

Over the past five years (2020-2025)：

Rural primary school student numbers plunged by 42.0%, and teaching staff decreased by 31.7%; in Heilongjiang, rural primary students plummeted by 62.2%, reducing the student-to-teacher ratio from 6.09 to 4.21; in Guangdong, the student drop was 20.1%, edging the ratio down from 15.31 to 14.36.

Between 2020 and 2025：

Full-time teachers in private preschools dropped by 36.5%; private primary and junior high schools lost 31.8% and 17.1% of their teachers; private senior high school teachers surged by 73.9%, climbing from roughly 266,000 to 462,500.

From the 2023 to 2025 academic years (in Shanghai’s Pudong New Area)：

Kindergarten enrollments fell from roughly 120,400 to 106,600; junior high school enrollments swelled by 15.9% to 154,700.

Between 2024 and 2025：

The number of regular primary schools nationwide fell from 136,300 to 128,300, a loss of about 8,000 schools in a single year; nine-year consolidated schools increased from 18,478 to 18,788.

2025：

Preschool enrollments dropped by 3.58 million year-on-year to 32.25 million, a decline of about 10%; primary school enrollments fell by roughly 4.06 million, or 3.8%. Junior and senior high school student populations grew by approximately 1.23 million and 1.17 million, respectively.

2025：

The number of full-time preschool and primary school teachers fell by 7.7% and 2.0% from the previous year, whereas junior and senior high school teaching staff grew by 2.2% and 3.0%.

In 2025：

Primary school enrollments in Beijing ticked up by 0.2%, while junior high school numbers jumped by 10.5%; Heilongjiang saw primary and junior high school students dropping by 7.0% and 4.0%, respectively; Guangdong primary school enrollments fell by 2.4% even as junior high school enrollments grew by 5.3%.

In 2025：

The total number of students in private senior high schools reached 6.01 million, a 2.1% year-on-year increase; new enrollments fell for the first time in six years, with only 2.04 million new students admitted, dropping by 82,300, or 3.9%, even as national aggregate high school enrollments grew by 3.7%.

In September 2025：

Six state departments launched the “Action Plan for the Revitalization of County-Level Senior High Schools.”

During the Guangzhou municipal legislative sessions in January 2026：

Hu Guosheng noted that the city’s private high schools fulfilled only 60.4% of their enrollment quotas in 2025.

In August 2026：

The Liaoning Provincial Department of Education announced that small-scale schools with fewer than 100 students would be guaranteed public funding calculated at a 100-student baseline.

In June 2026：

The State Council incorporated cross-phase resource planning into its 15th Five-Year Plan for Educational Development.

In September 2026：

Pudong’s localized 15th Five-Year Plan for education was issued.

In October 2026：

Wei Yi analyzed the choices of 164 private high school students in a research brief.
