Beyond the Rose Garden and the military review, Trump and Xi get down to business

SCMP | FREE | 1251 words | 2026-09-25

After weeks of frustration, delays and posturing, Chinese President Xi Jinping and US President Donald Trump got down to business on Thursday, kicking off their high-stakes summit, Xi’s first on US soil since 2015.

The two started the morning with a formal arrival ceremony on the White House South Lawn and Rose Garden that included nearly 500 military personnel, the Chinese and American national anthems played by the US Marine Band and opening remarks from both leaders.

Both leaders sought to find areas of common ground, shared history and cultural touchstones, citing shared efforts during World War II.

If they focused on common interests, Trump said, there was much the two nations could achieve, citing people-to-people connections that “even made us allies in World War II”.

He promised to work with Xi “as our people worked together to win that war”.

Trump also said the two leaders would discuss pressing issues concerning security, technology and “superintelligence”, a new term he has touted to describe artificial intelligence.

“The decisions we make in these areas today can promote peace and prosperity for decades and decades to come,” he added.

Xi followed in a similar vein, inviting 100,000 young Americans to visit China, offering a pair of giant pandas to Zoo Atlanta and calling for greater cooperation.

“I am ready to work with you to steer the giant ship of the China-US relationship on a steady course towards the future,” Xi said at a lectern in front of US and Chinese flags.

“We should strengthen communication,” he added. “China and the United States are different in national conditions, but through candid, in-depth and continued dialogue, our two countries can understand each other better.”

Xi gave a nod to Trump’s “America first” catchphrase even as he emphasised the importance of recognising China as an equal, underscoring the messaging by both leaders to their respective domestic audiences.

“Achieving the great rejuvenation of the Chinese nation and making America great again can go hand in hand,” he said.

“Let us meet the people’s expectation and show our historical initiative. Let us keep advancing the cause of friendship between our two peoples, and work together to build a better world.”

Their comments were followed by a military review in the Rose Garden, part of which Trump has cemented over, capped by a flyover of B-2 Spirit bomber and F-22 Raptor aircraft before the pair settled down for bilateral meetings behind closed doors.

China’s first lady, Peng Liyuan, and US first lady Melania Trump also attended the ceremony, attracting much attention during the event before they broke off to tour the National Museum of Asian Art.

Beneath the florid language and soothing statements of friendship are a host of critical issues that the two will work to hash out away from the cameras.

Analysts expect few breakthroughs or substantive deliverables this week on such difficult issues as artificial intelligence safeguards, rare earth minerals, trade tariffs, Taiwan arms sales, the Iran war, Ukraine and bilateral military relations.

But relative to last year’s escalating tariffs and harsh rhetoric, they welcomed signs of some sort of floor, however wobbly, under the freighted relationship.

One deliverable of sorts emerged hours before Xi’s aircraft even touched down – US Treasury Secretary Scott Bessent announced that the two sides would extend a trade truce reached nearly a year ago in South Korea, but only for two months.

In far loftier and substantive comments than his US counterpart, Xi touched on military communication, fentanyl, AI, investment and the need for more contact between each country’s lieutenants “in charge of foreign affairs, economic matters, financial cooperation and law enforcement and people-to-people exchanges”.

The nations’ militaries could “maintain regular dialogue” as part of an overall relationship that involved “healthy” competition that was “kept within bounds”, he said.

“We have both the capability and responsibility to develop and manage AI for good and ensure that the development of AI is always under human control and serves the well-being of the people,” the Chinese leader added. “We hope Chinese companies are treated fairly here in the United States.”

The talks are the heart of the three-day trip and followed Trump’s unusual move of greeting Xi in person at the foot of the jetway as he descended on Wednesday evening at Joint Base Andrews after a 15-hour flight, an expression of Trump’s respect for and desire for respect from the Chinese leader.

The last time a US president showed similar deference was in 2015 when then president Barack Obama greeted the pope. The time before that stretches more than a half-century back to 1962 when then US president John F. Kennedy met then British prime minister Harold Macmillan.

But the world has come to expect the unexpected from Trump, a proud disrupter who relishes slamming through norms, upending structures and unabashedly displaying his power and US military might.

Summit planners have worked hard to craft a meeting that mirrors Trump’s summit in Beijing four months ago. The time on the ground will be limited, with no side visits outside the capital but it will include a state dinner.

The carefully choreographed visit is in part meant to underscore respect and the equality of the two wary competitors, an issue of particular importance to Beijing.

But in sharp contrast to China’s highly systematic preparatory approach on display during their summit in Beijing in May, the lead-up to the US summit has been marked by less than ideal planning by a distracted US administration.

While the White House has been juggling its Iran war of choice, a Canadian trade showdown, Trump’s record-low popularity and pending November elections, summit preparation has also been frustrated by the small number of US decision-makers and an impetuous president inclined to rip up plans on short notice, according to sources.

Under other US administrations, intense summit planning started up to six months in advance, with deals and deliverables largely decided weeks ahead of the date.

With low expectations for deliverables this time, some movement on artificial intelligence is high on the agenda, as reflected in Xi’s introductory comments. The issue has been a source of rising tensions in recent months as the two nations tout competing visions, promote rival tech national champions and work in different regulatory environments.

Another entrenched problem of particular concern to the US side is Iran. Trump badly wants Beijing’s help to force Tehran to the bargaining table to end a war he started over six months ago. But China, which had been buying more than 80 per cent of Iran’s exported crude before the conflict, has balked at increasing the pressure on its Middle Eastern partner, although it has stopped short of supporting it in obvious military ways.

Another key issue involves the 90 per cent of global rare earth mineral processing that China controls. In recent months, Beijing has dispensed these critical resources sparingly to US companies.

China unsheathed its highly effective cudgel after “Liberation Day”, when Trump threatened tariffs of over 80 per cent on the Asian export giant. This has given China enormous leverage over the US and fundamentally changed the power dynamic between the world’s two largest economies, clout that China is in no hurry to give up.

“China-US cooperation may not solve every problem in the world,” Xi said on Thursday. “But without our cooperation, it would be hard to solve many of the world’s problems.
