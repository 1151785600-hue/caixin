China’s Communist Party promoted 10 civilian officials to full Central Committee membership at a Beijing leadership meeting, again passing over several higher-ranked alternates and appointing none from the military. The appointments were announced in a Xinhua communiqué on Oct. 29 after the fifth plenary session of the 20th Central Committee (Oct. 26–29), attended by 162 full members and 117 alternates. The third such round since the 2022 congress—the previous two elevated 14 alternates—it again skipped higher-ranked alternates despite constitutional provision for promotion by vote order, and no military alternates were promoted. All 10 appointees come from civilian institutions, four at full provincial rank and six at deputy provincial- or ministerial rank.

Shanxi Governor Lu Dongliang, 52, is the youngest full Central Committee member and China’s youngest official at full provincial- or ministerial-level rank. Born December 1973 in Liaoning, he spent years at Aluminum Corp. of China before entering provincial government in 2020, rising through Shanxi posts to become governor in June 2025. He is the second 1970s-born official to gain full membership, after Wei Tao, whom he displaced as youngest; both reached full provincial rank in summer 2025.

Bao Gang, 57, chairman of Inner Mongolia’s regional government, is an ethnic Mongol from Liaoning who has spent his entire official career in the region. He succeeded Wang Lixia—who had fallen from power—in October 2025. With Bao’s promotion and Wang’s expulsion from the party, ethnic Mongol full members remain at two.

Zhejiang Governor Liu Jie, 56, began his career in the steel industry and has held posts in Hunan, Jiangxi, Guizhou and Zhejiang. In 2016 he became the first official born in the 1970s to join a provincial party standing committee; he became governor in January 2025.

Xing Shanping, 58, deputy party secretary of Shaanxi, has held united front, publicity and organization posts on provincial standing committees in Shandong and Fujian before taking her current post in May 2024. With her promotion and Wang Lixia’s expulsion, the number of women holding full Central Committee membership remains at 14.

Liu Qiang, 55, deputy party secretary of Shandong and party secretary of its capital Jinan, spent much of his career in finance, including as Bank of China vice president, before moving into government in 2018, where he served as Shandong vice governor and standing committee secretary-general. He became Jinan party secretary in March 2022 and was promoted to Shandong deputy party secretary in July 2026 while retaining the Jinan post.
