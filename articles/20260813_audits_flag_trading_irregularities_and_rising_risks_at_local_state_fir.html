Audits Flag Trading Irregularities and Rising Risks at Local State Firms

Caixin Global | FULLTEXT | 320 words | 2026-08-14 01:37

As of 2025 budget execution reports：

Reports underscore growing operational strains at regional state firms, with Beijing, Jiangxi, and Zhejiang identifying operational and financing risks as major concerns.

By the end of 2025：

12 companies had yet to transfer their required shares, while 61 of 190 companies that had completed the transfer paid no dividends, as noted in 2026 audits.

Before 2026：

Recent audits in 15 Chinese provincial-level regions began revealing financial mismanagement, unauthorized trading, and rising investment risks at local state-owned enterprises.

As of Thursday in 2026：

15 provincial-level regions released annual fiscal audit reports for the previous year, highlighting unauthorized financing and trading to inflate revenue.

In 2026：

In Shandong, seven state-owned enterprises engaged in unauthorized commodity trading or made non-core investments totaling 92.1 billion yuan.

In 2026：

In Hubei, four subsidiaries of a state firm lent 860 million yuan through trades lacking commercial substance to boost revenue.

In 2026：

Zhejiang flagged 520 million yuan in irregular trading exposure at risk of losses.

In 2026：

Reports pointed to weak investment discipline, including inadequate due diligence and poor oversight.

In 2026：

In Shandong, 29 projects in strategic emerging industries, involving 7.7 billion yuan, were progressing slowly or failed to meet return targets due to insufficient feasibility analysis.

In 2026：

In Gansu, two state-owned companies strayed from national priorities for overseas expansion and the Belt and Road Initiative, leading to prolonged work stoppages and poor management.

In 2026：

In Hubei, state firms left large areas of land, hundreds of thousands of square meters of property, and nearly 300 pieces of equipment unused.

In 2026：

In Yunnan, one state-owned enterprise failed to refinance 610 million yuan of debt, leading to lawsuits and account freezing after an 83-million-yuan default.

In 2026：

In Qinghai, auditors warned that eight local state firms exceeded the national ceiling for liability-to-asset ratios.

In 2026：

Several provinces cited state enterprises for failing to remit required profits and state capital gains.

In 2026：

Guangdong highlighted delays in transferring state capital to replenish the national social security fund.
