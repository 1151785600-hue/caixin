In Depth: Former Shanxi Richest Man Indicted on Mafia, Casino Charges

Caixin Global | FULLTEXT | 583 words | 2026-08-06 08:34

Early 1994：

Zhang Xinming partnered with the Armed Police Gold Command to establish Shanxi Huabei Gold Industry Group Co.

1998：

Zhang Xinming registered Shanxi Jinye Coal and Coking Group Co. Ltd. under his brother's name after the military partnership dissolved.

2003：

Zhang Xinming acquired mineral rights to the Daning Jinhai coal mine, defeating major state-owned rivals.

2005：

Jinye Group's net assets surpassed 5 billion yuan, placing Zhang Xinming on the Hurun China Rich List.

September 2005：

Wu Yuan, former head of Taiyuan Commercial Bank, was sentenced to 17 years in prison; Zhang Xinming was implicated in bribing him.

2007 to 2014：

Zhang Xinming and his brothers allegedly fabricated employment relationships to fraudulently obtain business travel permits for gamblers and junket operators.

2009：

A provincial anti-corruption task force investigated Zhang Xinming for tax evasion, but the probe vanished after being submitted to Jin Daoming.

2009：

The Daning Jinhai coal mine's value surged to over 10 billion yuan; Zhang Xinming demanded his shares back.

Late 2009：

China Resources Power Holdings began negotiating to acquire Jinye Group's assets.

2010：

Another provincial task force investigating Zhang Xinming's alleged mafia ties concluded the accusations were unfounded.

2010：

Zhang Xinming launched lawsuits over the Daning Jinhai mine equity, including the case that led to Supreme People's Court Judgment No. 76.

February 2010：

China Resources and Jinye Group agreed to a deal where China Resources would pay up to 7.9 billion yuan for an 80% stake.

Spring and summer of 2010：

Zhu Liang accumulated 180 million yuan in gambling debts through side-betting arrangements; Zhang Xinming's syndicate allegedly used intimidation to collect.

2012：

An equity dispute involving Zhang Xinming's Shanxi Jinhai Energy Co. Ltd. reached the Supreme People's Court; Zhang Xinming approached Xi Xiaoming's son with a 30 million yuan bribe.

August 2012：

The Supreme People's Court issued a favorable ruling for Zhang Xinming (Judgment No. 76); he delivered the 30 million yuan bribe in two installments.

Late 2012：

The 18th Communist Party National Congress began, launching a sweeping anti-corruption campaign.

February 27, 2014：

Jin Daoming, a former senior provincial legislature official, was investigated, marking the start of systemic corruption revelations in Shanxi.

May 2014：

Zhang Xinming gave a high-profile interview denying collusive ties with China Resources Chairman Song Lin.

August 2014：

Zhang Xinming was taken away by armed police in Taiyuan, beginning a long period of public silence.

2014：

A journalist publicly accused China Resources Chairman Song Lin of intentionally leaking state assets to Zhang Xinming; Song Lin was investigated days later.

2017：

Xi Xiaoming was sentenced to life in prison for accepting more than 114 million yuan in bribes.

April 2020：

Zhang Xinming was sentenced to four and a half years in prison by a court in Changzhou, Jiangsu province, for crimes including corporate bribery and loan fraud.

August 2020：

Zhang Xinming was released upon completing his prison sentence.

February 2023：

The Supreme People's Court issued a civil ruling addressing the case tied to Xi Xiaoming, upholding the original judgment.

Around February 2023：

Zhang Xinming reappeared in public, acting as a litigation agent for his son in the Supreme People's Court.

2023：

The Daning Jinhai mine equity disputes were retried; the Supreme People's Court upheld Judgment No. 76 but ruled against Zhang Xinming in parallel disputes.

Spring of 2024：

Zhang Xinming attempted to broker a truce with old adversaries, confiding he was being secretly investigated.

November 4, 2024：

Zhang Xinming was taken away by police in Jincheng on suspicion of extortion and operating casinos.

December 10, 2024：

Zhang Xinming was formally approved for arrest.

Late June 2026：

The Jincheng Municipal People’s Procuratorate indicted Zhang Xinming on 17 charges, including organizing and leading a mafia-like organization.
