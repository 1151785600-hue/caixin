Zhejiang University Issues Model AI Law as China Moves Toward National Legislation

Caixin Global | FULLTEXT | 806 words | 2026-10-19 08:31

1. Zhejiang University legal scholars have released a proposed 99-article AI law draft that would require human review of AI-assisted medical decisions, give consumers the right to challenge consequential automated decisions, and impose record-keeping duties on developers and providers

2. The proposal addresses disputes over whether AI companies may use artists' works to train models, liability when large language models produce false answers, and how people can pursue claims after being targeted by AI-generated face-swapping videos

[para.

4

]

. The eight-chapter document calls for oversight spanning an AI system's full life cycle—from development and deployment to operation and withdrawal—and establishes rules for high-impact uses including health care, justice, education, and finance, while barring applications that manipulate users' emotions or exploit vulnerable groups

[para.

5

]

.

3. At the center of the proposal is the "capability-intelligence-task" principle, according to Sun Xiaoxia, head of Zhejiang University's Institute of Digital Rule of Law and leader of the research team

[para.

6

]

. The framework assesses whether a system's operational capabilities, cognitive maturity, and the nature and consequences of a particular task are properly matched

[para.

6

]

. The assessment would help determine whether a system is suitable for a task, conditionally suitable, or unsuitable, providing a basis for tiered regulation, market access, and liability allocation

[para.

7

]

. "The risk of a task depends not only on whether AI is powerful enough, but also on whether it can perform the task reliably and whether the task is appropriate to delegate to it," said Li Wenlong, who led drafting of the proposal

[para.

8

]

.

4. The draft rejects a blanket approach to liability for erroneous AI outputs, instead holding developers, service providers, and deployers responsible within the scope of their actual control

10

]

. Liability would be determined by considering the system's intended use, known limitations, foreseeable risks, and whether necessary safeguards were taken—not judged solely by output accuracy

[para.

10

]

. The draft also states that a company couldn't argue it had met all obligations simply because a human supervisor was nominally involved

[para.

11

]

.

5. For copyright disputes over model training, the draft requires developers to establish a lawful basis for use, honor rights holders' restrictions on use, and retain records on data sources and processing

[para.

12

]

. In health care, AI-generated diagnostic conclusions, treatment plans, and medication recommendations could only be used after review by a licensed physician

[para.

13

]

. Financial institutions using AI to make unfavorable decisions must provide a channel for human review with a clear processing deadline, rather than offering only generic explanations

[para.

]

. Individuals harmed by AI outputs could file appeals with providers or deployers, which would have 15 working days to process complaints

6. The proposal promotes access to AI development resources, requiring public computing platforms to offer access on fair, reasonable, and nondiscriminatory terms

[para.

[para.

16
