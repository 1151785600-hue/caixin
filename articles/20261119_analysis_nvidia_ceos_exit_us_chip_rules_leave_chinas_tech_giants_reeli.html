Analysis: Nvidia CEO’s Exit, U.S. Chip Rules Leave China’s Tech Giants Reeling

Caixin Global | FULLTEXT | 637 words | 2026-11-20 10:57

1. The ousting of Nvidia CEO Jensen Huang and new U.S. export control rules cutting remote access to advanced AI chips have dealt a major blow to China's largest tech companies. Executives were surprised by the speed of restrictions, leading one major company to halt Southeast Asia cloud computing talks and assess unwinding deals, as it had been renting AI data center capacity primarily in Singapore and Malaysia.

2. The U.S. Commerce Department's Bureau of Industry and Security announced an interim final rule on Monday, effective Thursday, to prevent Chinese companies from circumventing export controls by remotely accessing advanced U.S. chips in foreign data centers. This threatens China's AI model industry, as domestic chips still lag Nvidia's, and training frontier models depends on Nvidia's advanced chips; domestic chips are mainly used for inference, not training.

3. Since China cannot import Nvidia's most advanced processors, many firms rent access through cloud providers in Southeast Asia, which has substantial compute capacity: Singapore 1.4 GW, Malaysia 1.06 GW, Indonesia 900 MW, Thailand 620 MW. New rules require U.S. export licenses for chip shipments to data centers in Malaysia, Singapore, Thailand, Indonesia, and Brazil. Data centers providing over 12 million aggregate TPP to Chinese companies—equivalent to about 760 H200 or 320 B200 GPUs—also need licenses, far below the scale used for training models like Llama 3.1.

4. The restrictions impose a heavy compliance burden on chip vendors and data center operators, who must now know ultimate users. Chinese-funded companies are the largest customers in Southeast Asia, and data center projects with Chinese backing account for 60–70% of approved projects in Thailand. Tencent, Alibaba, and ByteDance lease data centers there; none responded to requests for comment.

5. The rules may have a loophole: Chinese companies could still access chips in U.S. data centers without triggering the new rule, though other restrictions may apply. Some executives believe the Trump administration may ultimately force Chinese firms to procure AI data center services directly in the U.S. Investor anxiety has hit Chinese AI model developers, with Zhipu AI shares down 13.4% and MiniMax down 18% on Tuesday.

6. The BIS move followed Huang's resignation over reports of extramarital affairs involving Alice Huang, a former executive at Megaspeed International, who has faced U.S. scrutiny over suspected Chinese intelligence ties. Huang's departure removes a key advocate against tighter chip controls; executives say China's model development has slowed, and the political environment is less favorable.

7. Congress may now revive stalled chip-control bills, including the Chip Security Act (tracking ultimate chip destinations) and the MATCH Act (pushing allies to restrict semiconductor equipment exports to China). Nvidia's interim CEO Colette Kress said the company stopped shipping Hopper data center chips to China, and its outlook no longer includes such shipments; it sold $2.4 billion of licensed Hopper products in the quarter ended Oct. 25.

8. Ultimately, the restrictions could accelerate domestic semiconductor development. Huawei is likely the biggest winner with Ascend chips, followed by Cambricon (strong price-performance for inference) and Hygon (easy migration from Nvidia's CUDA). Chinese chipmakers captured about 41% of China's AI accelerator market in 2025, up sharply, while Nvidia's share fell to 55%. DeepSeek's V4 is designed for Huawei's chips, and Alibaba, ByteDance, and Tencent have placed bulk orders of Huawei processors.

9. Executives say the timetable for domestic advancement has accelerated; what was a two- or three-year goal now needs immediate achievement. While the U.S. may gain a short-term compute advantage, China is being irrevocably pushed toward a "domestic model plus domestic chip" system, which could limit the impact of U.S. restrictions once established.
